EPA 9: Providing anaesthetic care for paediatric patients aged between 3 to 16 years old

Specifications

  1. Assessing the child pre-procedure and recognising the need for optimisation of medical condition, relevant preoperative investigation, and premedication for anxiolysis
  2. Counselling and obtaining informed consent from legal guardian, and assent from the child as appropriate, for sedation/ general anaesthesia using age- appropriate language
  3. Formulating and implementing an anaesthetic plan that considers the child’s physiological, psychological, and surgical condition including depth of hypnosis required, mode of induction, fasting duration, venous access, fluid management, airway management, temperature management, pain management, choice of appropriate monitoring, equipment and drugs, and emergence and recovery from general anaesthesia/ sedation
  4. Initiating management of common anaesthesia related complications/ crisis such as laryngospasm, and calling for expert help appropriately
  5. Assessing and managing acute pain in children and referring complex pain patients to a paediatric pain consultant
  6. Providing initial age-appropriate resuscitation in a critically ill child including trauma
  7. Providing anaesthetic care for a child in a remote location such as a diagnostic imaging suite
  8. Communicating information effectively with relevant health care team members through verbal and accurate documentation

Limitations

This EPA does not include the following:

  1. Neonates, infants, and children aged less than 3 years old
  2. Children ASA 4 and above
  3. Children with congenital heart disease, potentially difficult airway or undergoing airway procedures, one- lung ventilation, neurosurgical emergencies

Sub-competencies: PC1, PC2, PC3, PC4, PC5, PC7, PC8, PC10, MK1, MK2, SBP2, PBL1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • History taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations
  2. Procedural skills
    • Management of paediatric airway
    • Intravascular access either direct or ultrasound guided
    • Simple regional anaesthetic blocks like penile and ilioinguinal nerve blocks
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient-centric communication including counselling and taking informed consent from parent/ legal guardian and using age-appropriate language for the child
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including team brief/huddling and accurate documentation
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management, time management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides efficient patient-centred compassionate care in a timely manner while ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Clinical rotation: min 4 months in JR (R1 to R3) and 2 months in SR (R4 to R5)
  2. Case Logs
  3. Simulation
  4. Teaching sessions/ workshops/ webinars

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increase in patient morbidity and mortality.
  2. Increase in medico-legal liability.
  3. Increased patient/family dissatisfaction
  4. Increase in healthcare costs

Entrustment questions for EPA 9

Mini-CEX — GA/ SR case, ASA 1-2 aged 3-10 years

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Counselling for informed consent
  2. Management of laryngospasm
  3. Pain Management options
  4. Management of postoperative agitation

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. If this patient had URTI, how would you counsel the parent?
  2. If after removal of the LMA deep and transferring the patient to the trolley, the oxygen saturation starts to fall to 95%, what are your differential diagnoses and what would you do?
  3. If the child starts crying in the recovery and is inconsolable, how would you manage the situation?

Mini-CEX — GA/IPPV case, ASA 2-3, age 3-5 years

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Management of anticipated/ unanticipated difficult airway (mask ventilation/ intubation)
  2. Strategies for nasal intubation
  3. Extubation strategy
  4. PONV
  5. Fluid management

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. What are the implications of OSA in young children? If this patient had a STUBR score of 3, how would that determine your management of the patient
  2. If your patient had to have nasal intubation and you encountered difficulty advancing the tracheal tube down below the cords, what would you do to circumvent the problem?
  3. How do you risk stratify for PONV in children and decide on the appropriate PONV prophylaxis for our current patient?
  4. What happens if your patient develops stridor post extubation?
  5. How are you going to manage fluids in this patient and why?

EBD — Paediatric trauma patient requiring surgery

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Assessment of paediatric trauma patient
  2. Management of paediatric trauma patient
    • a. Modified RSI
    • b. Fluid management
    • c. Airway management, emergency
    • d. Drug doses
    • e. Massive haemorrhage
  3. Paediatric APLS- discussion regarding resuscitation
  4. Differences in paediatric trauma vs adult trauma

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. How would you assess a paediatric trauma patient and what in particular would you ask in the history from the caregivers?
  2. What is modified rapid sequence induction and how would you perform that?
  3. If this patient were a 5-year-old who had been hit by a car and arrives at ED with a GCS of E3V3M3, HR of 160 and BP of 70/40mmHg with a CT scan that shows an extradural hematoma
    • a. How would you conduct the anaesthetic for this patient?
    • b. If there is difficulty getting another peripheral venous line for this patient, how would you proceed?
    • c. What would your anaesthetic goals be?

EBD — Special needs child requiring anaesthesia

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Preoperative assessment and preparation of special needs child
  2. Role of premedication
  3. Role of care giver in perioperative management
  4. Management of lines and tubes postoperatively
  5. Prevention of iatrogenic injury

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. If you saw this child in the pre-anaesthetic clinic, how would you counsel the parents and what can you do to better prepare the child for surgery and make the perioperative experience a better one for parent and child?
  2. If we were to give premedication to this child, what would your choice of drugs be and the route? Are there maximum doses you need to be mindful of?
  3. What is our emergence strategy going to be for this child and why do you say that?
