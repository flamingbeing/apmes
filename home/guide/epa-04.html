EPA 4: Assessing and managing patients with potentially difficult airway in the hospital setting

Specifications

  1. Identifying patients with features of any of the four categories of anticipated difficult airway – bag mask ventilation, intubation, supraglottic airway, front of neck access; and assessing risk of aspiration
  2. Discussing risks with the patient and obtaining informed consent
  3. Planning and executing management options for ventilation, oxygenation, intubation, extubation and post-operative care of patients with potentially difficult airway including ensuring optimal patient positioning
  4. Managing an awake intubation
  5. Formulating back-up airway management plans in accordance with difficult airway algorithms
  6. Preparing the operating theatre, equipment, drugs, and personnel required in the management of difficult airway
  7. Communicating concerns and coordinating multidisciplinary care of patients with an anticipated difficult airway
  8. Seeking assistance from colleagues more experienced in the management of difficult airways
  9. Facilitating surgery requiring a shared airway
  10. Managing difficult airway in locations outside of main operating theatre.

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following

  1. Unanticipated difficult airway scenarios

Sub-competencies: PC1, PC3, PC5, PC7, MK1, MK2, SBP2, PBL1, PBL2, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Knowledge

  1. Difficult Airway Algorithm guidelines (e.g., Difficult Airway Society (DAS)/ ASA) for intubation and extubation, including obstetrics and paediatrics Difficult Airway Algorithms
  2. Airway physiology
  3. Anatomy of airway, neck, and chest
  4. Interpreting airway investigations including imaging such as X-ray, CT scan and MRI to aid airway management
  5. Airway management devices e.g., nasal, and oral airway, first and second generation supraglottic airways, different types of endotracheal tubes, tracheostomy tubes, flexible bronchoscope, airway exchange catheters, bougies, optical stylets, laryngoscopes
  6. Devices to facilitate oxygenation e.g., high-flow nasal cannula, jet oxygenation/ ventilation
  7. Techniques to optimise oxygenation through appropriate positioning, pre-oxygenation, and apnoeic oxygenation
  8. Knowledge of airway equipment available in institution-specific difficult airway trolley
  9. ‘Front of neck access’ techniques and indications
  10. Pharmacology of drugs used during airway management including neuromuscular blockers

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • Recognising features of a potentially difficult airway by history, examination, and review of pertinent investigations
    • Assessing adequacy of airway patency and ventilation
    • Identifying and correcting problems and complications associated with airway management
  2. Procedural skills
    • Use of airway adjuncts including nasal and oral airways
    • Use of supraglottic airway devices as rescue device and conduit for flexible bronchoscopy assisted intubation
    • Use of different intubation techniques including use of video-laryngoscopes and flexible bronchoscopy
    • Use of intubation adjuncts such as bougie and stylet
    • Use of shared airway techniques including use of micro-laryngeal tubes and jet ventilation
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient-centric communication including counselling and taking informed consent
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including team brief/huddling and accurate documentation
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides patient-centric compassionate care, ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Multispecialty anaesthesia rotation including Ear nose throat (ENT) surgery
  2. Case logs for airway surgery
  3. Residency programme teaching/Difficult airway training session including Cannot Intubate Cannot Oxygenate (CICO) scenario management
  4. Institution code activation for emergency airway management

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increased patient morbidity/dissatisfaction/ mortality
  2. Increase in healthcare costs
  3. Increased medico-legal liability

Entrustment questions for EPA 4

Mini-CEX — Anaesthetic management of a patient with anticipated difficult airway

Discussion points / expectations

  • Obtains a comprehensive assessment of a potentially difficult airway
    • History, examination, and review of pertinent airway investigations including nasoendoscopy pictures, X-ray, CT scan and MRI
    • Features for each of the four categories of anticipated difficult airway - bag mask ventilation, intubation, supraglottic airway, front of neck access
  • Understands and remembers key components of the Difficult Airway Algorithm by the Difficult Airway Society
    • Techniques for intubation
    • Bag mask ventilation
    • Uses of supraglottic airway
    • Equipment needed and steps for front-of-neck access
  • Knowledge of options for airway management devices according to Mini-CEX scenario, the advantages and limitations, including micro-laryngeal tube and jet ventilation
  • Formulates an airway management plan and communicates it to the relevant multidisciplinary teams
    • Explains choices made in management plan
    • Appropriately articulates anticipated concerns with the patient’s airway
    • Coordinates airway management plans in a responsible and collegial manner
  • Able to explain or justify the choices made in the airway management plan
  • Prepares for potential complications
    • What if Plan A fails?
    • What would resident do in the event of equipment failure while managing the airway?
  • Appropriate counselling of patient with regards to airway management concerns and plans
    • Demonstration of cultural competence in communication
    • Attention to detail in documentation
  • Knowledge of airway equipment available in institution-specific difficult airway trolley and in airway bag
  • Demonstrates professionalism and reflects on performance, seeks avenues for improvement.

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What features in this patient suggest a difficult airway/what do you think your greatest challenge is in having to manage this airway?
  2. What is your management plan for this patient?
  3. What if things had not gone according to your plan?
  4. What circumstances would prompt you to consider an awake option for airway securement?
  5. What guidelines for difficult airway management are you aware of?
  6. What considerations would you have with respect to extubating this patient e.g., if this were a grade 4 larynx that you had great difficulty intubating, what would your extubation strategy be?

Mini-CEX — Anaesthetic management of a patient going for surgery with a shared airway

Discussion points / expectations

  • Objectives of anaesthesia in a shared airway procedure
    • Methods to safely secure airway
    • Surgical requirements for airway in this particular type of procedure
    • Options for maintenance of anaesthesia for this specific type of shared airway procedure
    • Considerations for extubation and techniques for safe extubation
    • If applicable, interpreting airway investigations e.g., nasoendoscopy pictures, X-rays, CT scans and MRI.
    • Knowledge of options for airway management devices according to Mini-CEX scenario, their advantages and limitations, including micro-laryngeal tube and jet ventilation
    • Formulate an airway management plan and communicate it to relevant multidisciplinary teams
    • Explain choices made in management plan
    • Appropriately articulates concerns with the patient’s airway
    • Coordinates airway management plans in a responsible and collegial manner
    • Able to explain the reasoning behind choices made in airway management plan
    • Able to troubleshoot intraoperative airway problems
    • What would the resident do if the airway device gets inadvertently dislodged during surgery?
  • Appropriate counselling of patient with regards to airway management concerns and plans
    • Demonstration of cultural competence in communication
    • Attention to detail in documentation
  • Timely and responsible handover of airway issues post-operatively to relevant teams
  • Demonstrates professionalism and reflects on performance, seeks avenues for improvement.

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. Why are the benefits of your chosen anaesthetic technique for this particular patient undergoing this particular surgery?
  2. What are the potential risks to the patient with regards to your chosen airway management and anaesthetic technique?
  3. Are there alternative airway management plans you would consider in this patient undergoing this particular surgery?
  4. How is jet ventilation performed?
  5. Would your airway management plans change if the patient were morbidly obese with a high risk of aspiration?
  6. What are the signs you would be looking out for that might suggest loss of control of the airway (e.g., airway has been dislodged), and what would you do?
  7. What are the steps to take if there is sudden high airway pressure during the surgery?

DOPS — Scope assisted tracheal intubation

Discussion points / expectations

  • Indications for scope assisted intubation
  • Preparation and utility of equipment used for awake scope assisted intubation
  • Appropriate counselling of patient with regards to airway management concerns and plans
    • Demonstration of cultural competence in communication
    • Attention to detail in documentation
  • Methods of airway topicalization including maximum doses
  • Options for sedation during awake intubation
  • Clear communication with patient throughout intubation process
  • Demonstrates professionalism and reflects on performance, seeks avenues for improvement
  • For detailed steps and expectations, please refer to the guide for DOPS

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. If blood obscures the scope image, what would you do and what would you tell the patient?
  2. What sort of sedation would you consider appropriate for this patient?
  3. What other methods of airway topicalization do you know of?
  4. Why were awake scope intubation chosen for this patient and when would this technique not be a suitable choice?

DOPS — For difficult airway intubation using three different types of laryngoscope blades (curved, hyper-angulated, straight) and/or different models of video-laryngoscopes

Discussion points / expectations

  • Advantages and disadvantages of different laryngoscope models and blade types
    • Quality of display
    • Ease of obtaining view
    • Ease of intubation
    • Availability of hyper-angulated or straight blades
    • Portability
    • Disposable or non-disposable parts
    • Battery operated or not
    • Cost
  • Laryngoscope models and blade types available in different locations in the hospital e.g., OT, ICU, radiology suites etc.
  • Demonstrates professionalism and reflects on performance, seeks avenues for improvement.

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. Can you tell me what alternative video-laryngoscopes you know of?
  2. Name 3 models of video-laryngoscopes and compare their advantages and disadvantages with what you are using today.
  3. What sort of situations should different types of laryngoscope blades i.e., curved, hyper angulated, straight blade, be used?
  4. How would you perform an awake intubation with a video-laryngoscope and when would you use such a technique?

EBD — Airway management of patient with a difficult airway, requiring alternative intubation approaches such as awake intubation +/- front of neck access

Discussion points / expectations

  • Decision making process – Plans A, B, C
  • Communicating with patient – risks, informed consent
  • Coordinating with teams – preparing OT and personnel, communicating concerns with multidisciplinary teams
  • Extubation strategy of an ‘at-risk’ airway
  • Resource and manpower management

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  • 50-year-old gentleman, 60kg, is a known case of nasopharyngeal carcinoma with previous radiation to the head and neck. He has well-controlled hypertension, cataracts, and some hearing loss, but no other chronic medical issues. He is now admitted for acute appendicitis requiring surgery.
    Airway assessment reveals a mouth opening of 2.5 finger-breaths and some limitation of neck movements. Previous anaesthetic records on EMR documented his larynx as Grade 1. Post rapid sequence induction, direct laryngoscopy reveals a Grade 4 larynx. How would you proceed?
