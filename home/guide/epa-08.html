EPA 8: Providing anaesthetic care for patients in a non-operating theatre setting

Specifications

Remote locations for procedures that require Anaesthesia care may include the following settings:

  • Diagnostic Radiology and Intervention Radiology Centre
  • Cardiac Catheterisation Laboratory
  • Radiotherapy Centre
  • MRI Suite
  • Endoscopy Centre
  • Ambulatory Surgical Centre
  • Burns Centre
  • Dental Centre

Patients who require anaesthesia care in remote locations can range from well patients who need sedation to tolerate a procedure, to patients who require anaesthesia support because they are in a higher risk category (e.g., endovascular clot retrieval for stroke).

  1. Evaluating suitability of patients for procedures in remote locations
  2. Formulating and executing anaesthesia plans for patients undergoing procedures and interventions in remote locations
  3. Coordinating and communicating between multidisciplinary professionals to facilitate patient care

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following:

  1. Procedures involving the heart and/or access to heart chambers

Sub-competencies: PC1, PC2, PC3, PC4, PC5, PC7, PC8, MK1, MK2, SBP2, PBL1, P1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Knowledge

  1. Diseases or conditions that require interventional procedures in remote locations
    • Liver diseases (intrahepatic cysts) and portal hypertension (requiring transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt, percutaneous radiofrequency ablation therapy)
    • Paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia (for radiofrequency catheter ablation)
    • Cerebral aneurysm (for endovascular intervention)
    • Brain tumour (for radiotherapy)
    • Biliary duct disease: Choledocholithiasis, biliary duct stenosis (for endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography and interventions)
    • Renal cyst
    • Burns injury
  2. Interventional procedure specifics including procedure related requirements and complications
    • Transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt for portal hypertension
    • Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography and interventions
    • Radiofrequency catheter liver cysts, renal cysts
    • Cardioversion
    • Cerebral endovascular interventions (hemodynamic targets)
    • Physics and physiologic effects of imaging modalities (magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan, intensive imaging scan, computerised axial tomography scan), types of radiation, and radiotherapy
    • Radiocontrast media (barium-based (orally); iodinated agents and gadolinium contrast media (intravenously)
  3. Specific features/ hazards of remote locations
    • Diagnostic Radiology and Intervention Radiology Centres, Cardiac Catheterisation Laboratories, Radiotherapy Centres
    • Magnetic resonance imaging suites
    • Endoscopy Centres
    • Ambulatory Surgical Centres
    • Burns Centres
    • Dental Centres
  4. Anaesthesia considerations for procedures in remote locations
    • i. Working space
      • Central gas supplies
      • Equipment available for monitoring, drug delivery, oxygen delivery, anaesthesia delivery warming fluid, resuscitation, airway management
      • Imaging table
      • Radiation safety
      • Safety guidelines (in magnetic resonance suite, for procedures requiring image intensification, and radiotherapy)
      • Environmental temperature control
      • Dental chair anaesthesia
      • Shared airway (in dental surgery, in prone position)
      • Skilled assistants
    • ii. Crisis management

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • History taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations
  2. Procedural skills
    • Management of airway
    • Intravascular access (venous/ arterial/ Vascath)
    • Insertion of devices within the nasal, oropharyngeal, oesophageal, bladder and gastric cavity for the purpose of monitoring, extraction of gases or bodily fluids and/or infusion of gases or fluids
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient/ family centric communication including breaking bad news, end of life discussions
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including team brief/huddling and accurate documentation
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides efficient patient-centred compassionate care in a timely manner while ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Clinical rotations involving remote locations
  2. Teaching sessions on remote anaesthesia
  3. Case logs of cases requiring anaesthetic care in remote location: Senior Residency (R4 to R5)

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increase in patient morbidity/dissatisfaction/mortality
  2. Increase in medico-legal liability
  3. Increase in healthcare costs

Entrustment questions for EPA 8

EBD — MRI

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Hazards in the environment and safety guidelines
  2. Anaesthetic options for procedure
  3. Crisis management

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. What are the safety concerns with working in the magnetic resonance environment?
  2. What safety features are in place
  3. What are the concerns with providing anaesthetic care for patients in this environment?
  4. If your patient is having a scan and the ECG shows a bizarre tracing, how would you respond?
  5. How is the monitoring equipment used in the MRI environment different from those in OT?
  6. If your patient developed severe hypotension after administration of gadolinium contrast, and the capnography showed a tracing consistent with bronchospasm with tachycardia, what would you do?
  7. What anaesthetic options are available for a patient undergoing a scan for MRI neck?
  8. What anaesthetic options are available for a patient undergoing abdominal and pelvic scan for delineation of suspected renal tumour?
  9. The radiologist requests for a “breath hold” in an intubated adult patient receiving an MRI scan of the abdomen. How would you perform this?
  10. The patient has a sudden cardiac arrest while inside the MRI tunnel. What would be your initial response?

EBD — Interventional radiology suite

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Hazards in the environment and safety guidelines
  2. Anaesthetic options for procedure
  3. Crisis management
  4. Knows the difference between non- operating room anaesthesia (NORA) and operating room anaesthesia (ORA)
    • Less trained staff and equipment immediately available.
    • Often no scavenging/N2O.
    • No dedicated post-GA monitoring area.

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. What are the safety concerns with working in the interventional radiology suite?
  2. Where are the radiation safety equipment (e.g., lead gowns) found in your institution?
  3. What investigations would you look out for in this patient knowing that radiocontrast has to be administered?
    What if the patient has type 2 diabetes and is on metformin?
  4. What are the anaesthetic concerns with a 60-year-old lady (background hypertension, hyperlipidaemia and mild Coronary Artery Disease) planned for coiling of a left PCOM artery aneurysm 2 days after a subarachnoid haemorrhage?
    • a. Patient needs to be immobile (GA, intubated)
    • b. Limited access to patient (needs good IV lines and Arterial line monitoring)
    • c. Hemodynamically stable induction
    • d. Avoid further bleeding while maintaining CPP (Target SBP 100-140mmHg)
    • e. Secure airway with ETT
    • f. Invasive monitoring
    • g. Maintain normocapnia and normothermia.

    The interventional radiologist suddenly reports that the aneurysm has ruptured. What do you do?
  5. What are the differences between non- operating room anaesthesia (NORA) and operating room anaesthesia (ORA)?
