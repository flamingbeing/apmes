EPA 6: Managing critically ill patients in the intensive care unit (ICU)

Specifications

  1. Identifying, stabilising, and resuscitating critically ill patients
  2. Triaging of patients for admission to ICU
  3. Coordinating interdisciplinary management and disposition of critically ill patients to ensure optimal patient outcomes with appropriate use of ICU resources and transitions of care
  4. Initiating and managing organ/system support in critically ill patients including
    • a. Central nervous system – Sedation, Intracranial pressure management
    • b. Cardiovascular system– Vasoactive medications, Point-Of-Care-Ultrasound (POCUS), FloTrac®, Pulmonary Artery catheter
    • c. Respiratory system – Mechanical ventilation /Non-invasive ventilation including High Flow Nasal oxygen.
    • d. Gastrointestinal system – Stress ulcer prophylaxis, Nutrition
    • e. Renal system – Renal Replacement Therapy
    • f. Infection control – Rational use of antibiotics
  5. Performing critical care procedures such as intra-arterial and central venous cannulation, vascular catheter insertion for dialysis
  6. Performing concise handover of care with pertinent issues highlighted
  7. Leading a multidisciplinary critical care team in patient care e.g., during resuscitation
  8. Communicating difficult news to patient/ family with sensitivity e.g., breaking bad news, poor prognosis, end-of-life discussions
  9. Practicing infection control principles i.e., patient isolation/ precautions, personal protective measures

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following:

  1. Patients aged less than 16 years’ old
  2. Cardiothoracic surgical intensive care unit and coronary care unit patients

Sub-competencies: PC3, PC5, PC6, PC7, PC9, MK1, MK2, SBP1, SBP2, SBP3, PBL1, PBL2, P1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • History taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations
  2. Procedural skills
    • Management of airway
    • Intravascular access (venous/ arterial/ Vascath)
    • Insertion of devices within the nasal, oropharyngeal, oesophageal, bladder and gastric cavity for the purpose of monitoring, extraction of gases or bodily fluids and/or infusion of gases or fluids
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient/ family centric communication including breaking bad news, end of life discussions
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including team brief/huddling and accurate documentation
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides patient-centric compassionate care, ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Fundamental Critical Care Support (FCCS)
    Basic Assessment and Support in Intensive Care (BASIC)
  2. Residency Core Intensive Care Medicine (ICM) lectures
  3. Site specific ICM teaching, Department Continuing Medical Education (e.g., Grand Ward Rounds, Journal Club, Morbidity and Mortality Rounds)
  4. Hospital Infection Control Teaching
  5. Relevant Simulation Teaching/Exercises

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increase in patient morbidity/dissatisfaction/ mortality
  2. Increase in patient’s family’s dissatisfaction/ distress
  3. Increase in medico-legal liability
  4. Increase in healthcare costs
  5. Increased risk of delay in care of other patients

Entrustment questions for EPA 6

Mini-CEX — Triage

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Able to appropriately triage and devise a management plan for patients referred to ICU team
  2. Able to effectively communicate with patients and family members in the following challenging scenarios:
    • a. Critically ill patients not suited for ICU admission
    • b. Critically ill patients requiring ICU admission
  3. Critically ill patients where further advanced life support measures are deemed futile, and extent of care needs to be delineated/established

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  • a. You are asked to review a 65-year-old female in the ED, who p/w RHC pain and vomiting. She has history of ischemic heart disease and AF. She is jaundiced, mildly confused, and has RUQ tenderness. She was given 4L 0.9% NaCl due to hypotension on presentation. Her current vitals: Temp 39 deg C, RR 30/min, SpO2 92% on NRM, HR 120/min AF, BP 88/48 mmHg
    Abdo ultrasound shows dilated CBD and enlarged gall bladder with thickened walls. How would you manage this patient?
    • ABC, Surviving Sepsis guidelines, broad spectrum antibiotics, source control with decompression and drainage of biliary tract.
  • b. A 56-year-old woman develops a spontaneous SAH in the ward. She has a GCS of 12 and requires transfer to a tertiary centre for neuro-intervention. Outline the clinical and organisational issues that need to be addressed pre-transfer.
    • Coordination and communication, preparation of patient, monitoring, equipment and drugs, personnel
  • c. Outline your principles of management in the transport of the critically ill patient.
    • Justification for transfer, stability for transfer, equipment, monitoring, patient preparation, communication, trained personnel
  • d. You are called to see a 69-year-old male in the general surgical ward who has become profoundly hypotensive (75/40 mmHg). He is POD 5 after palliative surgery for a perforated malignant gastric ulcer. He is barely rousable and SpO2 is 85% on facemask 10L/min. Discuss your plan of management.
    • Information regarding his prognosis and expressed wishes, “comfort measures” should only be introduced when considered appropriate (with input from family and primary team), treat underlying cause of deterioration,
  • e. As part of ICU Outreach and stepdown review, you are seeing a 72-year-old lady with history of severe COPD who was just discharged from the ICU yesterday. She was admitted for cholangitis and decompensated type 2 respiratory failure and had 3 weeks of difficult ICU management and prolonged ventilator weaning process. According to her daughter, the patient lives independently but is limited by severe exertional dyspnoea. What are the principles of managing her should she deteriorate and require admission to ICU?
    • Pillars of ethics: beneficence, non-maleficence, justice, autonomy, futility/utility.
    • Prepare for ACP discussion esp. with regards to patient’s desire for intubation, if necessary, in the future, the use of NIV and other invasive organ support etc.
    • Decision can be made to limit or withdraw therapy if therapy: (1) is unlikely to succeed (physiologically non-plausible), (2) is known to fail in vast majority of times under such conditions and/or associated with adverse consequences, (3) will lead to a QOL that is unacceptable, (4) lead to preservation of state that is unacceptable for the patient
  • f. That night, the patient develops mucus plugging and becomes drowsy. Her daughter demands for ICU admission. What will you do?
    • Depends on results of fam conf and any plans reached.
    • Assess appropriateness and other alternatives.
    • Treat reversible causes – chest physio, nebs saline, supplemental oxygen etc.

    Align with patient’s wishes to preserve autonomy

DOPS — Arterial line

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Knows appropriate indication/ contraindication for arterial line insertion.
  2. Knows the appropriate set up and components of the measurement system
  3. Able to interpret information provided by the arterial tracing
  4. Troubleshoot commonly encountered device-related complications including site bleeding, hematoma, and difficult insertion etc.
  5. Appropriate consent taking- explains indication, risks, benefits
  6. Correct patient, indication, checks for no contraindications
  7. Communicates with patient during procedure to ensure comfort, uses LA
  8. Appropriate checking of equipment including ultrasound machine if indicated and zeroing transducer
  9. Appropriate aseptic technique
  10. Proper technique, feels for pulse before attempt
  11. Talks to patient throughout procedure
  12. Communicates clearly with healthcare team
  13. Escalates appropriately to senior when unable to handle
  14. Institutes appropriate patient monitoring
  15. Demonstrates situational awareness during procedure
  16. Manages and disposes sharps safely
  17. Appropriate securement of IA line
  18. Documents procedure properly

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What are some indications of arterial line insertion?
  2. What are the components of the arterial BP monitoring system?
    (IA catheter, fluid filled tubing, fluid in tubing, counter pressure fluid bag, pressure transducer, signal conditioning and monitoring software)
  3. What information can be gained from an arterial line trace?
    • Measurements: HR, systolic pressure, diastolic pressure (coronary filling), MAP (systemic perfusion), pulse pressure, pulse pressure variation, slope of anacrotic limb (associated with AS)
    • Waveform shape: Slope of anacrotic limb represents aortic valve and LVOT flow, slurred wave in AS, collapsing wave in AR, low dicrotic notch in states with poor peripheral resistance, position, and quality of dicrotic notch as a reflection of the damping coefficient)
  4. How would you assess damping/ dynamic response in the arterial pressure transducing system? What do you understand by a square wave test?
  5. How would the arterial waveform differ at the various sites of insertion?
    Peripheral arterial waveforms have a higher systolic BP, steeper systolic upstroke, lower diastolic BP, lower and later dicrotic notch and wider pulse pressure compared with measurements obtained at the aortic root.

DOPS — Dialysis catheter

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Knows appropriate indication/ contraindication for dialysis catheter insertion.
  2. Able to justify the choice of site and length of catheter used
  3. Troubleshoot commonly encountered device-related complications including site bleeding, hematoma, inadvertent arterial cannulation, and poor lumen flows etc.
  4. Appropriate consent taking – explains indication, risks, benefits
  5. Ensures no contraindication to dialysis catheter placement
  6. Communicates with patient during procedure to ensure comfort, uses LA
  7. Appropriate monitoring
  8. Appropriate checking of equipment, including ultrasound machine and transducer
  9. Strict aseptic technique
  10. Knows landmark technique in addition to using ultrasound guided placement
  11. Proper technique including pre-procedure scan to identify sonoanatomy
  12. Checks the guidewire position on ultrasound after insertion by scanning in transverse and longitudinal planes
  13. Ensures dilation of track does not result in guidewire kinks
  14. Manages and disposes sharps safely
  15. Appropriate securing of dialysis catheter with 3-point suturing
  16. Ensures all lumens are freely patent, heparin locked and capped
  17. Documents procedure properly
  18. Orders and checks chest x ray post line insertion
  19. Communicates clearly with healthcare team members
  20. Escalates to senior appropriately when unable to handle
  21. Demonstrates situational awareness during procedure

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. Outline the anatomical structures relevant to the insertion of a femoral dialysis catheter.
  2. How would you differentiate the vein from the artery on ultrasound?
  3. How would you choose the site of insertion and length of dialysis catheter to be used?
  4. What are some of the complications associated with dialysis catheter insertion? How can you minimise these complications?
  5. At the end of insertion of the dialysis catheter and while flushing it, you find that there is resistance in aspirating and flushing one of the ports. What do you think are some possible reasons and what would you do?
  6. You had just inserted a dialysis catheter for a patient. However, on clearing the procedure trolley, you realised that the guidewire had not been removed. How would you manage this situation?
  7. After you had dilated and inserted the dialysis catheter into the vessel, bright red blood backflows out from the lumens. Describe your immediate next steps in management.
  8. 3 hours into initiation of CRRT, the nurse informs you that access pressures are high. How would you troubleshoot this?
  9. A repeat episode of high access pressure occurs despite your initial management steps. The nurse then proceeds to run the CRRT on reverse flow. What do you understand by “reverse flow” and how would it impact dialysis?
  10. The dressing around on the dialysis catheter is heavily soaked. On inspection of the insertion site after removal of the dressing, there is oozing around the puncture site. How would you manage this?

EBD — Post general surgery critically ill patient

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Understand the basic principles and areas of postoperative care for a post-operative patient admitted to the ICU
  2. Management of common post-surgical complications e.g., AKI, ileus, high drain/NG output etc.
  3. Checklist for extubation
  4. Know the indications and contraindications for commencing enteral or parenteral, and associated complications

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. A 60-year-old woman has a right hemi-hepatectomy for invasive cholangiocarcinoma. She has been admitted to the ICU for post-operative care. What are the problems she may develop in the first 48 hours and how would you treat them?
    • Related to major abdominal surgery + (2) specific for hemi-hepatectomy
    • Respiratory, CVS, bleeding, GI failure, liver failure, renal – HRS, ATN, CNS – analgesia, delirium, hepatic encephalopathy, metabolic – high lactate low phosphate, hypoglycaemia, hypercatabolic state, coagulopathy
  2. What are the indications for TPN post major abdominal surgery?
  3. Describe how you would order TPN.
  4. A 67-year-old man is admitted to the ICU following laparotomy for faecal peritonitis. He developed multi organ failure over the next 2 days, requiring increasing ventilator and inotropic support. He is oliguric, acidotic and has increasing serum creatinine.
    What are the likely mechanisms for this patient’s renal failure?
    When would you start dialysis for this patient?
  5. How would you assess fluid balance in the critically ill patient?
    • Clinical, fluid balance chart, daily weights, CVP. TTE, IVC assessments, SVV/PPV, CO monitor, bioreactance technology
  6. How would you assess readiness for extubation?
    What is a spontaneous breathing trial (SBT)?
    What are some ways you can perform an SBT?
    Over what duration should it occur?
    What do you understand by the Rapid Shallow Breathing Index (RSBI)?

EBD — Neurosurgical critically ill patient

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Recognition of the clinical signs, and basic management of raised ICP
  2. Acute management of seizures
  3. Prevention, surveillance, and treatment of vasospasm in SAH
  4. Goals of management of TBI
  5. Able to recognise clinical features of and work up for SIADH, diabetes insipidus, cerebral salt wasting
  6. Brain death certification and ICU management of the brain-dead organ donor

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. A 52-year-old patient is admitted to NICU for WFNS IV SAH following an aneurysmal rupture. On day 4 of admission, you notice that the patient has become hyponatraemic (Na 126 mmol/L). What are your differential diagnoses of the acute hyponatremia?
    Outline your principles of management for his hyponatremia and specific management based on the most likely diagnoses.
    • Ddx: SIADH, CSW, hypovolemic hypoNA, hypoadrenalism, hypothyroidism, hypervolemic hyponatremia, inappropriate IV fluids
    • SIADH most likely > CSW
    • Tx: Hypertonic saline, aim > 135. Cannot fluid restrict as she is at risk of vasospasm. Check RP q4-6h. Avoid hypotonic fluids. Check urine osmolality to help guide fluid administration.
  2. You are called to review a 55-year-old female following emergency clipping of a ruptured left MCA aneurysm which was completed 3 hours ago. She is intubated and ventilated, with an EVD in situ. She developed frank blood in the EVD and her ICP increased to 57 cmH2). What would be your approach to her initial management?
  3. A 45-year-old male is admitted to the ICU following severe traumatic brain injury. He is sedated, intubated, and ventilated.
    • What are your goals of management with respect to the TBI?
    • What is “secondary brain injury”?
    • What are the steps you would take to avoid it?

    This patient had an ICP monitor inserted. The initial ICP is 32 mmHg. How would you manage the raised ICP?
  4. Can you outline the ICU management of a 35-year-old patient who has fulfilled brain death criteria and is awaiting surgery for organ donation?

EBD — Critically ill patient with severe medical comorbidities

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Able to devise a reasonable management plan for a critically ill patient, taking into account severe medical comorbidities.
  2. Interpretation of cardiac output monitors to guide hemodynamic management
  3. Theoretical and practical knowledge on basic TTE views and how to diagnose the different types of shock (hypovolemic, obstructive, cardiogenic)
  4. Strategies for management of ARDS, including advanced ventilation modes and PEEP titration
  5. Understand the basic components in kidney replacement therapy prescription and how to vary them to achieve a clinical target
  6. Display reasonable clinical judgment on transfusion triggers for the various cell lines, taking into account patient background and current medical state
  7. Management of common medical emergencies e.g., status asthmaticus, status epilepticus and thyroid storm etc.

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. A 55-year-old female is admitted to your ICU with severe respiratory failure caused by a community acquired pneumonia. She has a history of rheumatoid arthritis. What are some factors related to her rheumatoid arthritis that would impact your ICU management?
  2. A 35-year-old female is brought into ED with severe acute asthmatic exacerbation. Despite continuous nebulised ventolin, she goes into respiratory failure and is intubated.
    • Describe your initial ventilator settings and explain the rationale for each of your choices.

    1 hour later the patient becomes hypotensive.
    • What are your differential diagnoses?
    • What do you understand by dynamic hyperinflation?
    • What are your next steps in management?
  3. What are some of the possible approaches to setting PEEP in a ventilated patient with ARDS?
    • FiO2/PEEP titration table, use of P-V curve, adjust to maximum static compliance, staircase recruitment maneuverer best PEEP 2.5cmH2O above derecruitment point, transpulmonary pressure
  4. A 32-year-old female is admitted to your ICU for first onset seizures. She had been given doses of IV midazolam and loaded with IV levetiracetam. Despite this she continued to have seizures with persistently depressed GCS. She was intubated using propofol and rocuronium. Currently in the ICU, she is on propofol infusion 100mg/h but continues to have intermittent seizures. Briefly outline your specific management with respect to the seizures.
  5. Briefly outline how you would manage a thyrotoxic crisis in the ICU.
