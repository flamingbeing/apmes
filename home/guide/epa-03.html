EPA 3: Providing anaesthetic care for patients under regional anaesthesia (RA)

Specifications

  1. Assessing patient suitability for RA including evaluating baseline neurological deficits
  2. Formulating a regional anaesthetic plan with consideration for the type of surgical procedure, and the risks/benefits of various options including local anaesthesia, peripheral nerve blocks (PNB), central neuraxial blocks, sedation and monitored anaesthetic care (MAC)
  3. Utilising standard monitoring equipment, procedure-specific equipment, and drugs
  4. Performing regional anaesthesia related patient safety checklist including ensuring correct block for the surgery to be performed, informed consent, optimal patient positioning and all other preparations in place
  5. Performing RA with attention to sterility, patient comfort and proceduralist’s ergonomic considerations
  6. Providing monitored anaesthesia care for patients who have been given RA block by anaesthetists/ surgeon
  7. Managing side effects and complications associated with RA technique including failed RA/ local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST)/ neuropraxia
  8. Formulating and implementing post RA care plan
  9. Communicating care plans with patients and inter-professional teams
  10. Documenting procedure including complications if any

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following:

  1. Patient aged less than 16 years old

Sub-competencies: PC1, PC2, PC3, PC4, PC7, PC8, PC10, MK1, MK2, SBP1, SBP2, SBP3, PBL1, PBL2, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Knowledge

  1. Basic science/ General
    • Anatomy of nervous system and eye
    • Neuromuscular physiology, haemostatic physiology
    • Physiological changes and monitors used during sedation
    • Pharmacology related to local anaesthetics and adjuncts, sedation medications, pharmacokinetics of drugs in epidural/subarachnoid space,
    • Physics and equipment for safe and accurate delivery of local anaesthetics (including ultrasound machine, needles, peripheral nerve stimulator)
  2. Guidelines for:
    • Management of local anaesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST)
    • Neurological complications associated with RA technique
    • RA technique in patients receiving anticoagulant/antiplatelet/antithrombotic agents
  3. RA technique specific:
    • General considerations and equipment for specific RA technique
    • Applied anatomy: surface anatomy, sonoanatomy for relevant blocks
    • Side effects and complications of RA technique
    • Types of blocks:
      1. Central neuraxial: subarachnoid block, epidural, caudal
      2. Cervical plexus
      3. Upper limb: brachial plexus (interscalene, supraclavicular, infraclavicular/ costoclavicular, axillary)
      4. Lower limb: lumbar plexus, fascia iliaca, femoral nerve, adductor canal, saphenous nerve, obturator nerve, sacral plexus, sciatic nerve, ankle block
      5. Truncal: transversus abdominis plane, rectus sheath, iliohypogastric/ ilioinguinal, paravertebral, erector spinae plane, intercostal
      6. Others: dorsal penile nerve, Bier’s block
      7. Ophthalmic blocks: subtenon, peribulbar, retrobulbar
  4. Surgery-related considerations:
    • Common surgical procedures performed under RA, including ophthalmic surgery, orthopaedic procedures with or without use of tourniquet

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills: history taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations
  2. Procedural skills
    1. Use of ultrasound machine (image acquisition, optimisation, interpretation), needles, catheters, peripheral nerve stimulator, continuous infusion devices
    2. Ability to correlate surface anatomy with sonoanatomy
    3. Perform specific RA blocks:
    4. Central neuraxial blocks: subarachnoid block, epidural block
    5. Brachial plexus blocks: interscalene/ supraclavicular/infraclavicular/axillary
    6. Lower limb nerve blocks:
      1. Anterior compartment e.g., Fascia Iliaca/femoral nerve block
      2. Posterior compartment e.g., sciatic block
    7. Truncal blocks: erector spinae plane/rectus sheath/iliohypogastric, ilioinguinal nerve/paravertebral/ transversus abdominus plane block
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient-centric communication including counselling and taking informed consent
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including team huddling and accurate documentation
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides patient-centric compassionate care, ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Clinical rotations:
    • Multispecialty anaesthesia rotations/core anaesthesia training that cover orthopaedic, general, urological, thoracic, vascular, gynaecology and ophthalmic surgeries.
    • Obstetric anaesthesia rotation (at least 4 months in Junior Residency (R1 to R3) and 2 months in Senior Residency (R4 to R5)
    • RA rotation during Junior Residency (R1 to R3) with a focus on peripheral nerve blocks (at least 1 month)
  2. Case Logs
  3. Teaching sessions

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increase in medico-legal liability
  2. Increase in healthcare cost
  3. Increase in patient dissatisfaction/ morbidity/ mortality
  4. Conversion to general anaesthesia
  5. Increase in overall duration of surgery leading to delay in care of other patients

Entrustment questions for EPA 3

Mini-CEX — Manage a patient for surgery under peripheral nerve block and sedation

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Sedation options
  2. Monitoring required
  3. EBD discussion points as above for DOPs

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. Explain your choice of sedation
  2. what would you do if 30 min later, the patient starts to get restless?

DOPS — Spinal/ subarachnoid block

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Contra-indications to block including management of anticoagulants/ antiplatelets/ antithrombotic agents
  2. Alternative approaches to performance of block
  3. Choice of local anaesthetic and dose
  4. Patient safety checklist

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What are the contraindications to subarachnoid block?
  2. If this patient were on aspirin/clopidogrel/apixaban, how would you proceed with the spinal?
  3. If you encounter difficulty getting into the subarachnoid space with this needle and keep hitting bone, how would you modify your technique?
  4. Explain your choice of spinal needle

DOPS — Upper limb block (single shot): Brachial Plexus

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Contra-indications to block
  2. Alternative approaches to performance of block e.g., addition of peripheral nerve stimulator
  3. Post block care instructions

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. How would you use a nerve stimulator to aid in performing this block?
  2. How would you recognise Horner’s syndrome/ diaphragmatic paralysis/ pneumothorax?
  3. If patient develops incomplete block, paraesthesia/ Horner’s syndrome/ diaphragmatic paralysis/ pneumothorax, what would you do?

DOPS — Lower limb block (single shot) Any one of the following blocks: Fascia iliaca; Femoral nerve block; Popliteal approach to sciatic nerve block

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Contra-indications to block
  2. Alternative approaches to performance of block e.g., addition of peripheral nerve stimulator
  3. Post block care instructions

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. If the patient develops foot drop that is noted during pain round the next day, how would you proceed in managing the patient?
  2. If the patient complains of persistent numbness, what would you do and how would you counsel the patient
  3. If a 40kg patient had 0.5% 20mls of bupivacaine given each for a femoral nerve block and sciatic nerve block and complains of tinnitus and giddiness soon afterwards, what would you do?

DOPS — Truncal block (single shot) Any one of the following blocks: Erector spinae block; rectus sheath block; Transversus abdominis plane block; ilioinguinal/ iliohypogastric nerve block

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Indications and contraindications for block
  2. Alternative to block

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What are the signs and symptoms of local anaesthetic systemic toxicity?
  2. How would you provide satisfactory surgical conditions if a patient wanted to do ilioinguinal herniorrhaphy under local anaesthetic only?

DOPS — Any peripheral nerve block with catheter

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Indications and contra-indications to block and use of catheter
  2. Alternative approaches to performance of block including additives
  3. Care of catheter and troubleshooting catheter related problems

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. If the patient develops pain from the nerve block which is not working satisfactorily intraoperatively, what would you do?
  2. If the patient develops neuropraxia post catheter removal, how would you manage?
  3. If there is difficulty flushing the catheter, what would you do?
  4. The patient/ ward staff note that there is leaking of fluid around the injection site, how would you manage this?
  5. When removing the catheter, what should you look out for?
  6. What instructions does the patient need to be told with regards to be having the catheter in place for post-operative pain relief?

EBD — Management of a patient undergoing ophthalmic surgery under MAC

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Sedation options
  2. Monitoring required

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. If the patient starts to panic when the drapes go over face, what would you do?
  2. If the surgeon wants to give a peribulbar block for the eye and as he does so, bradycardia develops. What do you need to consider and proceed to do?

EBD — Management of a patient undergoing surgery under subarachnoid block

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Sedation options
  2. Monitoring required
  3. choice of local anaesthetic and dose

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. How would you recognise a high block/ total spinal?
  2. Are there alternatives to spinal block for this patient?
  3. Patient develops hypotension/ inadequate block/high block/total spinal/post dural puncture headache, what would you do?

EBD — Management of a patient undergoing surgery under subarachnoid block/single shot peripheral nerve block

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Sedation options
  2. Monitoring required
  3. Alternative approaches to performance of block e.g., addition of peripheral nerve stimulator
  4. Post block care instructions

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What if the patient under sedation becomes restless halfway through the surgery?
  2. What is your analgesic plan for the patient to avoid analgesic gap?

EBD — Management of patient undergoing surgery with a peripheral nerve block catheter

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Indications and contra-indications to block and use of catheter
  2. Alternative approaches to performance of block including additives
  3. Care of catheter and troubleshooting catheter related problems

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What are the advantages of inserting a catheter for peripheral nerve block in this patient?
  2. If this patient were very scared of having the block done awake, how would you proceed?
  3. What post-operative care instructions would you give and how would you ensure that the information is conveyed to the patient and ward staff?
  4. If you were the senior resident on call and your junior resident calls you in because there is resistance on catheter removal, how would you proceed?
