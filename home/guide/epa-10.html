EPA 10: Managing adult patients for cardiovascular procedures and interventions

Specifications

  1. Performing risk assessment and optimising medical comorbidities prior to interventions
  2. Preparing equipment and medications for cardiovascular procedures and interventions with attention to maintaining sterility of invasive vascular lines
  3. Communicating care plans with anaesthesia and interprofessional teams.
  4. Providing perioperative care of patients for cardiovascular procedures and interventions.
  5. Providing sedation and intravenous anaesthetics for interventional procedures.
  6. Managing crises
  7. Transferring patients to post-anaesthesia care unit or intensive care unit.
  8. Conducting handover of patient care.

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following:

  1. Patients aged less than 16 years old

Sub-competencies: PC1, PC2, PC3, PC4, PC5, PC6, PC7, PC8, MK1, MK2, SBP2, PBL1, PBL2, P1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Knowledge

  1. Basic science
    • Anatomy and physiology of the cardiovascular system
    • Applied anatomy of vascular access for invasive monitoring
    • Guideline-directed medical therapy for cardiovascular diseases.
    • Pharmacology of medications used during cardiovascular procedures and interventions.
    • Equipment (anaesthetic machine, monitoring, and airway equipment)
  2. Investigative tools
    • Coronary angiogram
    • Sestamibi (MIBI) scan
    • Stress tests (pharmacologic, exercise)
  3. Monitoring tools
    • Transoesophageal echocardiography
    • Monitoring equipment (Interpreting waveforms and parameters on different types of hemodynamic monitoring devices e.g., equipment for measuring cardiac output, pulmonary artery catheter)
    • Cerebral monitoring (e.g., cerebral oximetry monitoring, processed EEG monitoring)
    • Coagulation profile (activated clotting time, thromboelastography, rotational thromboelastometry)
  4. Cardiopulmonary support
    • Mechanical ventilation (Indications, modes of ventilation, complications, maintenance, lung recruitment manoeuvres, weaning and extubation criteria.)
    • Humidification (indications, modes, complications)
    • Pacemaker (temporary and permanent; implantable cardioverter defibrillator)
    • Extracorporeal circuit (cardiopulmonary bypass circuit, veno-venous and venoarterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenator circuits)
    • Types of mechanical circulatory support
  5. Organ protection
    • Cardioprotection
    • Neuroprotection (cerebrovascular accident, postoperative delirium, postoperative cognitive dysfunction)
    • Spinal cord protection in repair of descending aortic aneurysm
    • Renal protection
    • Thermoregulation (indications, modes, complications)
  6. Cardiovascular procedures and interventions
    • Coronary artery grafting surgery (on pump, off-pump)
    • Open-heart procedures
    • Transcatheter aortic valve implantation
    • Radiofrequency ablation of atrial fibrillation procedure
    • Percutaneous closure of atrial septal defect
    • Surgical repair of aorta (ascending, arch of, and descending)
  7. Institution-specific processes
    • Timeout, sign-in, and sign-out processes.
    • Institution-specific massive blood transfusion protocol
    • Antibiotic prophylaxis
  8. Cardiovascular diseases or disorders
    • Hypertensive disease
    • Diastolic dysfunction
    • Ischaemic heart disease
    • Stable chronic heart failure
    • Cardiac arrhythmias
    • Types of aortic aneurysm
    • Aortic dissection
    • Types of pulmonary hypertension
    • Pulmonary thromboembolism
    • Vasoplegia
    • Low cardiac output syndrome
  9. Gastroenterology
    • Gastric oesophageal reflux disorder
  10. Haematology
    • Anaemia
    • Thrombocytopenia
    • Coagulation disorders
    • Systemic inflammatory response
    • ABO-incompatibility
  11. Nephrology
    • Cardiac surgery associated acute kidney injury
    • Chronic renal failure
    • Electrolyte disturbances
    • Discoloured urine
  12. Neurology
    • Seizures
    • Cerebrovascular disease (e.g., amaurosis fugax, transient ischaemic attack, stroke)
  13. Infection control
    • Central venous catheter associated infection
    • Deep sternal wound infection

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • History taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations
  2. Procedural skills
    • Management of airway
    • Intravascular access (central venous/ arterial)
    • Ultrasonography skills
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient-centric communication including counselling and taking informed consent
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including team brief/huddling and accurate documentation
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management, time management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides efficient patient-centric compassionate care in a timely manner while ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Completion of 12 weeks of cardiothoracic anaesthesia module.
  2. E-learning modalities
  3. Transoesophageal echocardiography simulation
  4. Case Logs

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increase in patient morbidity/ dissatisfaction/mortality.
  2. Increase in medico-legal liability.
  3. Inappropriate over-utilisation of healthcare resources/ increase healthcare cost

Entrustment questions for EPA 10

DOPS — Central Venous catheter (CVC)

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Appropriate consent taking- explains indication, risks, benefits
  2. Understands indications and contraindication to CVC placement
  3. Communicates with patient during procedure to ensure comfort, uses LA
  4. Appropriate monitoring during insertion including ECG
  5. Appropriate checking of equipment, including ultrasound machine and transducer
  6. Appropriate aseptic technique
  7. Knows landmark technique (accurately locates anatomical landmarks) in addition to using ultrasound guided placement
  8. Proper technique
  9. Checks the guidewire position on ultrasound after insertion
  10. Ensures that direction for dilation of track does not result in bent guidewire
  11. Communicates needs clearly with healthcare team members
  12. Escalates to senior appropriately when difficulty arises
  13. Demonstrates situational awareness during procedure
  14. Manages sharps appropriately and safely disposes of them
  15. Appropriate securing of CVC
  16. Documents procedure properly
  17. Orders and checks chest X ray post CVC insertion

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. Describe the anatomy, surface marking, landmarks for internal jugular venous and subclavian venous routes of CVC insertion.
  2. Identify the structures in the ultrasound scan you have acquired.
  3. What are the complications associated with insertion of CVC?
  4. What if the patient developed atrial arrhythmias after insertion of the guidewire?
  5. After the drapes are removed, the patient is noted to be extremely pale, the heart rate has increased from 80/min to 95/min and the blood pressure has decreased from 160/90mmHg to 100/78mmHg. What do you think has happened and what would you do next?
  6. What would you look out for on the chest X ray after CVC insertion.

EBD — Patient undergoing cardiac surgery requiring CPB

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Discuss methods of cardiology investigations and evaluations, and interpretation and perioperative application of findings of these investigations.
    • a. treadmill exercise
    • b. Dipyridamole - 99mTechnetium sestaMIBI (methoxybutyl isonitrile) scan
    • c. cardiac CT calcium score aka coronary calcium scan
    • d. coronary angiogram
  2. Discuss CABG
    • a. How to manage preoperative and intraoperative period up to commencement of bypass.
    • b. How to manage a haemodynamically unstable patient
    • c. Indications for cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) or off CPB CABG
    • d. Pharmacokinetics of heparin/ protamine
    • e. What is heparin resistance and heparin induced thrombocytopenia and how do you overcome these conditions?
  3. Interpretation of echocardiography images
    • a. Evaluation of volume status and ventricular contractility.
    • b. Detection of air in the heart chambers.
  4. The principles of pacing the heart.
    • a. Pacing modalities and codes
    • b. What mode and at what rate that makes sense for patients who develop new onset dysrhythmia in non-cardiac surgery settings
    • c. Precautions necessary for a patient with pacemaker/cardiac implantable electronic device undergoing non-cardiac surgery
  5. The principles and interpretation of thromboelastography (TEG) and rotational thromboelastometry (ROTEM)
  6. Management of surgical drainage of pericardial effusion
    • a. Describe the pathophysiology of cardiac tamponade.
    • b. Describe the investigations.
    • c. Describe the perioperative preparation and management for pericardiocentesis.

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What are the objectives of the treadmill exercise and how can you apply the findings to clinical practice.
  2. What is the MIBI scan and how is it done? What implications are there for risk stratification and CABG?
  3. If the patient has had cardiac calcium CT score aka coronary calcium scan, what are the implications of the findings?
  4. In this patient’s coronary angiogram, can you identify the main coronary arteries and sites of blockage? What implications are there for us?
  5. Can you explain critical stenosis of an artery? How does a critical stenosis affect the flow in the distal vascular bed at rest?
  6. When would interventional procedure be considered for a patient with coronary artery disease?
  7. If acute ST depression is noted on the ECG during surgery, how would you manage the patient?
  8. How would you manage the patient’s haemodynamic responses just before aortic cannulation and during CPB for this patient undergoing CABG?
  9. What organ protective strategies can be employed in this patient during CPB
  10. What conditions are required to wean a patient off CPB?
  11. Which patients would benefit from a trans-oesophageal echocardiography in a non-cardiac setting. Would you be able to demonstrate the different views in this patient and tell me what you see?
  12. What precautions are necessary for a patient with pacemaker/cardiac implantable electronic device undergoing non-cardiac surgery?
  13. Interpret this ROTEM/TEG printout and describe your management?
  14. How do you recognise cardiac tamponade?
  15. After CABG, this patient becomes acutely unstable while being transferred from the OT table to the bed. What would your management be?
  16. How would you manage patients for pericardiocentesis (drainage of pericardial effusion to relieve tamponade)?
