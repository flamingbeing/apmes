EPA 1: Performing preoperative/ pre-procedural assessment and optimisation of adult patients

Specifications

This EPA includes both elective and emergency procedures/ surgery.

  1. Establishing rapport with patients
  2. Gathering data on disease processes and medical issues relevant to anaesthetic care
  3. Identifying the need for, ordering, and interpreting appropriate pre-procedural investigations and following up of results
  4. Performing anaesthetic risk assessment and determining fitness for surgery
  5. Optimising patients preoperatively with inter-professional teams
  6. Referring patients to other specialties for further evaluation and optimisation as necessary
  7. Counselling and obtaining appropriate informed consent for anaesthetic care
  8. Counselling patients who are breastfeeding regarding anaesthesia and analgesia options
  9. Organising and documenting all relevant information to facilitate effective handover
  10. Proposing an appropriate anaesthetic management plan based on preoperative assessment

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following:

  1. Patients less than 16 years’ old
  2. Obstetric patients

Sub-competencies: PC1, MK1, MK2, SBP2, SBP3, PBL1, PBL2, P1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Knowledge

  1. Basic medical conditions, related complications and implications for anaesthesia including:
    • Cardiovascular
      1. Hypertension and ischaemic heart disease
      2. Valvular heart disease
      3. Rhythm disorders and conduction defects
      4. Cardiac implantable electronic devices
      5. Cardiac failure and cardiomyopathy
      6. Cardiac tamponade and pericardial effusion
      7. Congenital heart disease
      8. Pulmonary embolism
    • Pulmonary
      1. Obstructive disease
      2. Restrictive disease
      3. Pulmonary hypertension
      4. Infection e.g., upper and lower respiratory tract infections
      5. Tobacco usage
      6. Obstructive sleep apnoea
    • Neurology/ Muscular
      1. Cerebrovascular accidents
      2. Epilepsy
      3. Parkinson’s disease/ cerebellar disease
      4. Dementia
      5. Spinal cord injuries
      6. Peripheral neurological disorders
      7. Primary muscle disorders
    • Renal
      1. Renal failure
    • Liver
      1. Liver failure
    • Endocrine
      1. Diabetes mellitus
      2. Thyroid disease
      3. Pituitary disease
      4. Adrenal disease
      5. Phaeochromocytoma
      6. Carcinoid syndrome
      7. Chronic steroid use
    • Haematology
      1. Anaemia
      2. Polycythaemias
      3. Clotting disorders
      4. Antiplatelets and anticoagulants
    • Cancer
      1. Disease, therapies, and toxicities
    • Anaesthesia related genetic conditions
      1. Pseudocholinesterase deficiencies
      2. Malignant hyperthermia
    • Others
      1. Obesity
      2. Immunocompromised patients
      3. Post organ transplant
      4. Geriatric patients
  2. Risk stratification
    • Impact of medicines and drug interactions in the perioperative period
    • Factors improving surgical and long-term outcomes
    • Risk stratification and scoring tools to stratify surgical and anaesthetic risks
    • Common risk assessment tools for various diseases and complications
  3. Risk mitigation
    • Risk mitigation strategies and appropriate interventions
    • Optimisation strategies for smokers
    • Role of prehabilitation and patient optimisation
    • Role of nutrition in the perioperative period
    • Principles of enhanced recovery pathways
    • Strategies to minimise use of blood products

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • History taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations
  2. Procedural skills
    • Ability to navigate electronic medical records and access best practice guidelines to guide optimisation of patient.
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills:
    • Patient-centric communication including counselling and taking informed consent
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including team huddling and accurate documentation
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., ability to organise, time management, resource management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides patient-centric compassionate care, ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Perioperative assessment of patients as part of operating theatre/ core anaesthesia training rotations – both in the emergency and elective setting
  2. Pre anaesthesia clinic rotation (minimum 2 weeks)
  3. Teaching session including simulated patient encounters

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Inadequate patient preparation for surgery/ procedures resulting in postponement or cancellation of procedure
  2. Increase in patient morbidity/ dissatisfaction/ mortality
  3. Increase in medico-legal liability
  4. Increase in healthcare costs

Entrustment questions for EPA 1

Mini-CEX — Preoperative assessment of a patient in the hospital setting e.g., preoperative clinic/ pre-anaesthetic evaluation clinic (PEC), ward

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Assessment of post-operative nausea and vomiting risk and strategies for mitigation
  2. Ability to differentiate between drug allergy vs side effect
  3. Evaluation of a patient with symptoms of an upper respiratory tract infection
    1. Differentiate between upper respiratory tract infection and allergic rhinitis
    2. Knowledge of potential complications
    3. Rationale for cancellation/ decision to proceed
  4. Transfer of relevant information within the healthcare system e.g., dental work, need for further evaluation/ investigations
  5. Counselling for anaesthetic techniques (knowledge of risks/benefits)

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. How would you assess the risk of post-operative nausea and vomiting for this patient?
  2. If this patient tells you of a drug allergy, how would you proceed?
  3. This patient reports having a runny nose that started 3 days ago
    1. What further questions would you like to ask?
    2. How would you differentiate between upper respiratory tract infection and allergic rhinitis?
    3. How would you counsel this patient with regards to anaesthetic risk?
  4. Can you describe one risk stratification tool for patients undergoing surgery?
  5. How would you ensure the transfer of information to the relevant healthcare team with regards to this patient’s care e.g., dental work, need for further evaluation/ investigations?
  6. What are the different types of anaesthetic modalities and how would you counsel a patient with regards to the benefits and risks for each modality?

EBD — Assessment of a patient with pre-existing respiratory disease

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Relevant clinical findings or investigations to assess risk of pulmonary complications; discussion of investigations aimed at assessing lung function; optimisation strategies of various respiratory diseases such as asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), pulmonary hypertension
  2. Risk assessment and optimisation of a patient with recent or ongoing lower respiratory tract infection
  3. Role of prehabilitation
  4. Optimisation strategies for smokers

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. Let us say the patient with COPD whom you are reviewing in clinic is planned for an elective procedure and he reports a recent reduction in effort tolerance. How would you manage this situation?
  2. What are the anaesthetic considerations for a patient with GOLD stage 2 COPD undergoing elective surgery?
  3. If this patient had asthma, how would you determine if the asthma control is optimised?
  4. What do you know about prehabilitation and the evidence for its use? Do you know how to make arrangements for your patient to undergo prehabilitation?
  5. If this patient had a recent lower respiratory tract infection, how would you optimise the patient for urgent surgery?
  6. If your patient were a smoker who is keen to reduce the risk of perioperative respiratory complications, how would you counsel with regards to smoking cessation?
  7. What do you know about vaping and its implications for anaesthesia?

EBD — Assessment of a patient with pre-existing cardiovascular disease

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Assessment and optimisation of various cardiac conditions such as hypertension, ischemic heart disease
  2. Knowledge of guidelines for further cardiac investigations and referrals e.g., new ECG changes, rhythm disorders
  3. Management of a patient with a recent myocardial infarction
  4. Perioperative management of patients on anticoagulants/antiplatelet agents
  5. Effect and pre-procedural treatment of anaemia

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. How would you manage if this patient presented to you in the pre-anaesthetic clinic with a blood pressure of 180/110mmHg?
  2. What risk assessment tools do you know that help stratify a patient’s cardiac risk and algorithms that guide the need for further workup in a patient with pre-existing heart disease?
  3. If this patient were on aspirin/ apixaban or warfarin, what would your perioperative management of these agents be?
  4. If this patient’s Hb were 9.5g/dl and you reviewed this in the pre-anaesthetic clinic, what would you do next?

EBD — Assessment of a patient with pre-existing endocrine disease

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Assessment of complications and optimisation of the endocrine disease in the patient being discussed
  2. Perioperative steroid coverage for patients with known or possible adrenal insufficiency
  3. Perioperative management of patients with poorly controlled diabetes/ perioperative management of patients on various hypoglycaemic agents
  4. Perioperative management of patients with thyroid disease

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. How would you assess and manage a patient with insulin dependent diabetes undergoing elective surgery?
  2. If this patient were on metformin or SGLT inhibitors, how would you counsel the patient with regards to taking these medications in the perioperative period?
  3. If this patient were on prednisolone 10mg om for 3 weeks what do the guidelines say about perioperative steroid coverage?
  4. What are the potential complications of patients with un-optimised hyperthyroidism/ hypothyroidism undergoing surgery?

EBD — Assessment of geriatric patient

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Assessment of geriatric patients that includes comprehensive evaluation of comorbidities, with emphasis on neurocognitive function, mental health wellbeing, sensory impairment, substance abuse, nutritional status and frailty, presence of advance care directives and end-of-life goals/plans
  2. Neurocognitive assessment such as mini-cog for dementia; assessment of decision-making capacity
  3. Evaluating baseline and current functional status by screening for activities of daily living and limitations in mobility such as using the Time Up and Go test (TUG)
  4. Knowledge of validated scoring systems for frailty and nutritional status; knowledge of strategies for optimisation
  5. Knowledge and management of chronic medications/ polypharmacy in perioperative period
  6. Knowledge of factors contributing to postoperative delirium and strategies for its mitigation

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. Define a geriatric patient
  2. What issues are geriatric patients more likely to face in the perioperative period and bearing this in mind, how would you counsel for anaesthesia or take informed consent?
  3. What is frailty and what tools do you know of to assess this?
  4. Are there interventions in the pre-operative period that may reduce the risk of postoperative delirium?

EBD — Assessment of morbidly obese patient (same patient can be used for EPA 2 DOPS mask ventilation/LMA or mask ventilation/ ETT insertion for obese patient)

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Screening morbidly obese patients for anaesthetic risk factors such as difficult airway, technical procedural difficulties, and optimising patient for conditions associated with obesity
  2. Evaluation and optimisation of associated conditions such as metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, respiratory disease, endocrine disease, liver disease, gastroesophageal reflux disease and venous thromboembolism
  3. Screening for obstruction sleep apnoea (e.g., STOP-BANG score) and obesity hypoventilation syndrome

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. How would you screen for obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) preoperatively?
  2. What is your preoperative management of a patient with known OSA coming for elective surgery? How would you ensure the patient is optimised for surgery?
  3. What are the anaesthetic concerns for a morbidly obese patient?
  4. How would you perform preoperative anaesthetic counselling for a morbidly obese patient?
