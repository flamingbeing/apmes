EPA 5: Assessing and managing patients with polytrauma in the perioperative setting

Specifications

  1. Recognising patients with life-threatening injuries who require immediate stabilisation and resuscitation through a structured tool such as Advanced Trauma and Life Support guidelines
  2. Gathering and interpreting information to determine diagnosis and priority goals
  3. Initiating and organising resuscitation of unstable patients based on urgency of the situation and resource availability
  4. Establishing clear leadership, either by recognising the team leader or assuming the leadership role as appropriate. Delegating task and responsibilities in an appropriate and respectful manner
  5. Adapting management plans as the clinical situation evolves and seeking assistance in situations that are complex or new
  6. Recognising the potential for pulmonary aspiration during airway management and demonstrating competency in the advanced airway skills, including managing difficult airway and consideration for neck/ spine/ facial/ airway injuries
  7. Demonstrating competency in haemodynamic resuscitation including skills in obtaining central venous access
  8. Demonstrating understanding of activation of massive blood transfusion protocol
  9. Formulating and executing an anaesthesia plan including monitoring, invasive lines, and postoperative disposition, that considers the complexity of the patient’s medical condition (e.g., head injuries)
  10. Communicating and coordinating patient care involving other professional disciplines
  11. Organising the transfer of patient within the hospital or between hospitals with proper documentation and handover of care
  12. Seeking and interpreting multiple sources of information to modify patient care for better outcomes
  13. Performing team debrief

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following:

  1. Obstetric patients
  2. Patients aged less than 16 years old

Sub-competencies: PC1, PC2, PC3, PC4, PC5, PC6, PC7, PC9, MK1, MK2, SBP1, SBP2, PBL1, P1, P2, ICS2, ICS3

Knowledge

  1. Knowledge of Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS), or equivalent protocols including the performance of a primary and secondary survey with consideration for specific patient factors including the elderly, the young, patients with high body mass index (BMI), pregnant patients and patients with concurrent medical conditions
  2. Management of specific patient groups including patients with severe head injury, severe burns, mass casualty (including use of biological or chemical or radioactive agents)
  3. Management of massive haemorrhage including classes of shock, complications related to massive blood loss including principles of fluid resuscitation, use of blood products and management of coagulopathy, acidosis, and temperature
  4. Management of complications of massive blood transfusion including storage lesions, transfusion reactions, coagulopathy, and application of viscoelastic haemostatic assays in management of coagulopathy
  5. Role of point of care ultrasound in management of trauma patient
  6. Pharmacology of agents used in resuscitation
  7. Team management and post-event debrief
  8. Safe transfer of patients within and across different institutions

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • History taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations including viscoelastic haemostatic assays
  2. Procedural skills
    • Advanced airway skills including familiarity with various airway devices/ front-of-neck access
    • Management of ventilator including lung protective ventilation strategy
    • Vascular access including intra-arterial line/central venous line/ intraosseous line
    • Needle thoracotomy
    • Point of care ultrasound
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient-centric communication including counselling and taking informed consent where possible
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including team brief/huddling and accurate documentation
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management, team management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides patient-centric compassionate care, ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others
  6. Demonstrates professional behaviour in complex and stressful situations
  7. Maintains duty of care and patient safety while balancing multiple responsibilities

Experiences

  1. Completed ATLS or acquisition of equivalent knowledge/ experience
  2. Case log of managing patients with polytrauma (at least 4 cases) over 3 years
  3. Teaching sessions relevant to polytrauma
  4. Minimum 8 case logs by end R5

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increase in patient dissatisfaction/ morbidity/ mortality
  2. Increase in medico-legal liability
  3. Increase in healthcare cost

Entrustment questions for EPA 5

EBD — Any trauma case (Management of head / neck trauma)

Discussion points / expectations

  • Management of head / neck trauma

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. How would you exclude cervical spine injury in this patient?
  2. What precautions will you take when inducing general anaesthesia in this patient and why?
  3. In this patient before induction, what would you specifically look out for in your assessment of this patient?
  4. If this patient had a penetrating neck wound, what would you do?
  5. How would you prevent secondary brain trauma? What are your haemodynamic goals?
  6. What would you do if you noticed that the airway pressure was elevated and there was evidence of subcutaneous emphysema after intubation?
  7. In patients on warfarin, what would be required for reversal?

EBD — Any trauma case (Management of thoracic/ abdominal trauma)

Discussion points / expectations

  • Management of thoracic/ abdominal trauma

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What are the goals of damage control resuscitation? Describe your fluid management.
  2. What is your choice of induction method and airway management for this patient and why?
  3. If soon after induction, the blood pressure plummets, what are your goals for management?
  4. What would you do during the laparotomy if the blood pressure is persistently low despite transfusion of 1 litre of blood and your noradrenaline requirements are creeping up?
  5. How would you decide if a chest tube is required pre-induction or post-induction?
  6. Explain how you would use the ROTEM results to guide your resuscitation.
  7. What is the definition of massive blood transfusion? What are the potential complications arising from massive blood transfusion?

EBD — Any trauma case (Management of pelvic/lower limb injury)

Discussion points / expectations

  • Management of pelvic/lower limb injury

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What are the means for haemorrhagic control pre-op and intra-op?
  2. What are the potential complications arising from such injuries?
  3. What is damage control surgery?
  4. What are the features for compartment syndrome?
  5. What would be your pain management strategy?
