EPA 2: Providing anaesthetic care for adult patients requiring general anaesthesia (GA)

Specifications

This EPA includes providing anaesthetic care for patients of all physiological and pathophysiological states undergoing elective and emergency procedures within the operating rooms that are otherwise not specified in the list of limitations below.

  1. Ensuring all medico-legal requirements related to the procedure and anaesthesia, such as informed consent and documentation, are fulfilled.
  2. Formulating the general anaesthetic plan and prioritising tasks to carry out plan.
  3. Communicating information effectively to all members of the healthcare team such as during a team brief/ huddle.
  4. Ensuring all preparations required to execute the plan are in place including the operating room, equipment, staff, and the employment of personal protective measures.
  5. Complying with patient safety practices such as peri-procedural checklist/ timeout.
  6. Managing increased risk of aspiration from delayed gastric emptying with measures such as rapid sequence induction.
  7. Using appropriate physiological monitors to guide patient care.
  8. Inducing and maintaining an appropriate state of balanced general anaesthesia.
  9. Managing the airway of patients at all levels of consciousness.
  10. Minimising harm to the patient, from iatrogenic causes or due to the anaesthetised state, procedure or underlying medical condition, including (not exhaustive) eye injuries, pressure injuries, burns, neuropathy due to positioning, hypotension due to medication, hypercarbia due to pneumoperitoneum, hypoxia due to one-lung ventilation, etc.
  11. Recognising and managing peri-procedural complications/crisis in a timely manner including calling for help appropriately.
  12. Reversing the state of general anaesthesia and managing the emergence of the patient.
  13. Handing over care of the patient appropriately to fellow anaesthetists, nursing staff in the post-anaesthesia care unit or staff in the intensive care unit.
  14. Reporting adverse events when indicated.

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following:

  1. Patients pregnant with a viable foetus.
  2. Patients aged less than 16 years’ old.
  3. Patient undergoing procedures involving the heart and/or access to heart chambers and/or cardiopulmonary bypass or equivalent.
  4. Patients undergoing procedures NOT within the operating rooms.

Sub-competencies: PC1, PC2, PC3, PC4, PC5, PC7, PC8, MK1, MK2, SBP1, SBP2, SBP3, PBL1, PBL2, P1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Knowledge

  1. Applied physiology relevant to maintaining the stable physiological state to minimise harm to the anaesthetised patient
  2. Applied pharmacology relevant to maintaining the state of balanced general anaesthesia with pain relief and the stable physiological state to minimise harm to the anaesthetised patient
  3. Implication of breastfeeding on administration of common anaesthetic and analgesic drugs
  4. Principles, application and troubleshooting of equipment used for anaesthetic care
  5. Applied anatomy relevant to perform procedures required for safe conduct of anaesthesia
  6. Management of acute and chronic medical conditions pertinent to the provision of general anaesthesia in the perioperative period
  7. Anaesthetic considerations regarding emergency procedures
  8. Surgical/procedurals plans, guidelines and protocols associated with the peri-procedural care of the patient, such as enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS), deep venous thrombosis prophylaxis etc.
  9. Diagnosis and management of adverse conditions, including (not exhaustive) patient specific conditions such high airway pressure, malignant hyperpyrexia, thyroid storm, myocardial ischemia, arrhythmias, hypotension, hypoxaemia, changes in neurological status; equipment or facility related condition such as ventilator failure, power failure, fire, that may arise during the periprocedural period
  10. Local system-based practice relevant for peri-procedural care of the patient, including informed consent, adaptations of the WHO surgical safety checklist, required documentations, handover procedures
  11. Institution specific adverse events/critical incident reporting systems

Additional:

  • For geriatric patients
    1. Physiological and pathophysiological changes associated with normal aging and frailty relevant to provision of anaesthesia
    2. Differences in pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic effects of medications commonly used in the perioperative period in geriatric patients compared to young healthy adults
    3. Drug interactions between polypharmacy for chronic diseases and anaesthetic agents
    4. Adverse perioperative events commonly associated with elderly patients such as postoperative cognitive dysfunction, postoperative delirium, skin injuries, cardiovascular or neurological ischemia
    5. Neurocognitive assessment such as mini-cog for dementia
    6. Medico-legal implications of neurocognitive impairment and consent for anaesthesia and surgery
    7. Role of rehabilitation
    8. Strategies to minimise postoperative cognitive dysfunction/ delirium
  • For bariatric patients
    1. Definition of morbid obesity
    2. Conditions associated with morbid obesity including metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, respiratory disease, endocrine disease, liver disease, gastroesophageal reflux disease and venous thromboembolism
    3. Obstruction sleep apnoea and obesity hypoventilation syndrome: implications for anaesthesia and perioperative management
    4. Anatomical, physiological, and pathophysiological differences associated with morbid obesity relevant to provision of anaesthesia.
    5. Differences in pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic effects of medications commonly used in the perioperative period in obese patients compared to healthy adults.
    6. Specific equipment designed for morbidly obese patients including operating table, Troop elevation pillow etc.
    7. Adverse perioperative events commonly associated with morbidly obese patients
    8. Anaesthetic considerations for bariatric surgery
    9. Physiological implications and anaesthetic considerations for laparoscopic surgery
  • For intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring (IOMN) which is used in surgeries like neurosurgery, ENT surgery, spinal surgery to avoid injuries to nerves
    1. Various modes of IOMN and rationale for their use intraoperatively
    2. Applied physiology and pharmacology relevant to maintaining the state of balanced general anaesthesia while facilitating IONM.
    3. Principles of troubleshooting when there is a decrease or loss of signal intraoperatively
  • For thoracic surgery requiring lung isolation
    1. Lung function tests and diagnostic imaging of respiratory system relevant to anaesthetic management
    2. Airway anatomy
    3. Respiratory disease and their management implications for anaesthesia
    4. Physiology of one lung ventilation
    5. Indication for one lung ventilation
    6. Methods of establishing one lung ventilation and managing complications such as hypoxia and impaired gaseous exchange associated with it
    7. Anaesthetic considerations for pneumonectomy/ lobectomy
    8. Anaesthetic considerations for thoracoscopic surgery
  • For neurosurgery
    1. Basic neurophysiology such as cerebral blood flow (CBF) and factors that affect it, including cerebral blood flow autoregulation, cerebral blood flow response to CO2 and cerebral metabolism
    2. Pathophysiological effects of brain injury and techniques of preventing or limiting brain injury including managing raised intracranial pressure (ICP), maintaining cerebral oxygenation and perfusion, decreasing cerebral oxygen requirements such as seizure prevention or sedation
    3. Effects of anaesthetic induction and maintenance agents on ICP and CBF
    4. Anaesthetic considerations for neurosurgical procedures

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • History taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations
  2. Procedural skills
    • Management of airway
    • Intravascular access (venous/ arterial)
    • Insertion of devices within the nasal, oropharyngeal, oesophageal, and gastric cavity for the purpose of monitoring, extraction of gases or bodily fluids and/or infusion of gases or fluids
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient-centric communication including counselling and taking informed consent
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including team brief/huddling and accurate documentation
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides efficient patient-centric compassionate care in a timely manner while ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Clinical rotation to operating theatre
  2. Service updates on equipment used for the provision of anaesthetic care
  3. Case-log of patients presenting for various elective and emergency procedures requiring general anaesthesia
  4. Teaching sessions for directed skills acquisition in areas of airway management and central venous access
  5. Teaching sessions on surgery specific anaesthetic considerations
  6. Courses or teaching sessions on patient safety and risk management systems, such as the Institute for Healthcare Improvement (IHI) courses or equivalent

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increase in patient morbidity/dissatisfaction/ mortality
  2. Increase in medico-legal liability
  3. Increase in healthcare costs
  4. Increase in overall duration of surgery leading to delay in care of other patients

Entrustment questions for EPA 2

Mini-CEX — Patient requiring GA with endotracheal tube

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Ensures all medico-legal requirements/ patient safety checks have been conducted before induction of GA
  2. Able to induce and maintain and reverse general anaesthesia in patient
  3. Able to intubate and extubate the patient safely
  4. Knows how to manage ventilation and oxygen related crisis during anaesthesia
  5. Performs a systematic handover of care to PACU

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. After intubation, how do you decide what ventilator settings to set/ justify your ventilation settings for this patient?
  2. When would you decide to use rapid sequence induction, and can you describe how you would conduct it?
  3. What is the role of cricoid pressure and demonstrate how you would show an anaesthetic assistant how to apply it. How much force is supposed to be applied?
  4. If during surgery you notice that the airway pressure keeps increasing or the high-pressure alarm goes off, can you tell me what you would do?
  5. What safety features does the anaesthetic machine have to prevent delivery of a hypoxic mixture to the patient?
  6. How would you interpret if the capnography shows XXX and what would you do?
  7. If you notice the end tidal CO2 rising rapidly, what would you do? What would your differentials be?
  8. How would you ensure that the patient’s oxygen saturation remains above 95% after extubation and in PACU?

Mini-CEX — Patient requiring GA with LMA (inclusive of entrustment discussion on management of crisis related to anaesthetic agent use e.g., anaphylaxis, malignant hyperthermia)

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Ensures all medico-legal requirements/ patient safety checks have been conducted before induction of GA
  2. Able to induce and maintain and reverse general anaesthesia in patient
  3. Able to justify the use of LMA and choose an appropriate type of LMA for procedure

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. Why did you choose to use LMA in this patient and why did you choose this particular type of LMA?
  2. What if after giving the antibiotic/ after the surgeon started cleaning with chlorhexidine solution, you noticed that the patient’s heart rate increased, and blood pressure dropped? What would you do next?
  3. How do you prevent hypothermia? Which patients would be at risk for hypothermia?
  4. If during the surgery, the patient suddenly starts to breathe rapidly and a ‘crowing’ sound is heard, what would you do?
  5. If you notice the patient’s tooth is loose after removal of LMA, how would you proceed?
  6. How would you recognise malignant hyperthermia and how would you manage it?

DOPS — Mask holding and LMA insertion (R1: normal patient; R2: morbidly obese patient)

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Counsel patient appropriately and takes informed consent
  2. Able to determine adequacy of airway patency
  3. Knows how to optimise mask holding and ventilation
  4. Knows appropriate indication/ contraindication for LMA in patient
  5. Knows the different types of LMA available
  6. Able to justify choice of LMA in this particular patient
  7. Communicates with healthcare staff appropriately
  8. Knows when to trouble shoot problems with insertion including change in technique, apparatus, or plan
  9. Ensures appropriate depth of anaesthesia before attempting insertion
  10. Knows how to assess correct placement of LMA
  11. Checks for cuff pressure and knows ideal cuff pressure
  12. Able to name at least 2 potential complications each for mask holding and use of airway adjuncts such as oral airway or nasopharyngeal airway
  13. Able to name at least 2 complications or risks associated with LMA
  14. Knows safe management of laryngospasm and regurgitation
  15. For the morbidly obese patient, demonstrates realisation that ventilation and oxygenation may be challenging as seen by choice of LMA and technique used to ensure proper placement and assisted ventilation

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. Describe how you will instruct a medical student on how to hold a mask and ventilate adequately. How will you know if your medical student is ventilating adequately or excessively?
  2. What are some of the complications from mask-holding or using the various airway adjuncts?
  3. How will you choose the type and size of LMA for your case?
  4. How would you teach a medical student to insert the LMA and how does what you describe help with the insertion?
  5. How do you know that the LMA is properly inserted?
  6. What to do if there is a leak detected in the circuit with the LMA in place?
  7. What is the acceptable cuff pressure for the LMA?
  8. What are some of the complications or risks of using the LMA?
  9. You have a young patient who is listed for emergency evacuation of uterus following incomplete spontaneous abortion. You induced general anaesthesia in your patient and successfully inserted the LMA. When the patient is moved down the OT and placed into a lithotomy position, you noticed large amounts of brownish fluid welling up from the drain tube (gastric channel) of the LMA and in her mouth. What will you do now? What are the possible triggers for this?
  10. If following skin incision, there is an inspiratory stridor from the patient and then no waveform on the capnogram. What has happened? What will you do now? How can you prevent this from happening?

DOPS — Mask holding and ETT insertion in normal airway (R1: normal patient; R2: morbidly obese patient (same patient can be used for EPA 1))

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Knows indications for ETT intubation in the patient
  2. Takes appropriate informed consent
  3. Does proper airway assessment and recognises a potentially difficult airway
  4. Ensures required equipment is prepared before induction of anaesthesia
  5. Knows the difference between a curve and straight blade and how each is supposed to be used
  6. Ensures adequate hypnosis and muscle relaxation before intubation attempt
  7. Ensures patient is oxygenated well before intubation attempt
  8. Communicates appropriately with healthcare team to facilitate good intubating condition
  9. Monitors patient and demonstrates awareness of patient’s response to intubation attempt
  10. Demonstrates correct intubation technique
  11. Knows when to abandon attempt and escalate when necessary
  12. Knows how to determine correct tracheal tube placement (clinical and other methods like carbon dioxide detection)
  13. Measures cuff pressure and knows ideal cuff pressure
  14. In a morbidly obese patient, demonstrates proper positioning of the patient (e.g., using a troop pillow), ensures adequate pre-oxygenation and uses 2 men ventilation technique, considers apnoeic oxygenation

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What are some types of patients who might be difficult to mask ventilate and why?
  2. If morbidly obese Santa Claus (with his enormous facial hair) shows up one night when you are on call for a GA, would you expect any problems with oxygenation and how will you get around the problem (aside from getting OT technical assistant to shave him)?
  3. Describe how you will instruct a medical student on how to intubate a patient with an appropriate size endotracheal tube and confirm correct position of endotracheal tube. How would you know if the endotracheal tube is at the level of the carina?
  4. Can you describe at least 5 different types of endotracheal tubes that are available in your hospital?
  5. Why did you choose this particular type of endotracheal tube? What are some of the advantages and disadvantages of this endotracheal tube (assessor to decide on at least one variety of the named endotracheal tubes above)?
  6. What are some of the complications of endotracheal intubation? How can you minimise these complications?
  7. You have a young ASA-1 patient who came for emergency laparoscopic appendectomy. You performed rapid sequence induction and successfully intubated the patient. When you tried to check the position of the endotracheal tube by auscultation, you find that you have difficulty ventilating even with high pressures. You are unable to hear any breath sounds. What are the possible causes? What will you do now?

DOPS — Placement of double lumen tube (DLT) and confirmation using both auscultation and bronchoscopy

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Takes appropriate informed consent with counselling for generic risks associated with DLT
  2. Performs adequate airway assessment
  3. Prepares and checks appropriate choice of equipment for airway including size of DLT and FOB, checking of cuffs of DLT
  4. Articulates reason for choice of DLT in patient for OLV
  5. Demonstrates knowledge of anatomy of tracheobronchial tree
  6. Able to troubleshoot equipment issues to optimise view of tracheobronchial tree
  7. Appropriate analgesia/ sedation
  8. Ensures good intubating conditions before attempting intubation
  9. Ensures usual ASA monitors in place during procedure
  10. Confirms appropriate position of tube with FOB
  11. Checks correct position via auscultation
  12. Demonstrates correct steps resulting in appropriate depth of insertion with minimal complications during insertion
  13. Knows how to manage the FOB and diagnose/ troubleshoot problems that result in inadequate view of the tracheobronchial tree
  14. Knows how to adjust if the DLT has been placed in the incorrect bronchus
  15. Measures cuff pressure of tracheal tube
  16. Effective communication with the healthcare team
  17. Seeks help appropriately if unable to rectify problems encountered
  18. Knows when to stop if the patient is not tolerating the intubation attempts

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. Can you describe the difference between a left and right sided DLT?
  2. How would you insert a right DLT and what do you need to note when using a right sided DLT?
  3. Intraoperatively, the patient desaturates to SpO2 80%. Describe your immediate management of this patient.
  4. If the patient show signs of difficult airway e.g., receding chin with limited mouth opening and neck extension, what are your concerns prior to insertion of DLT? Are there any airway adjuncts/equipment that you would like to standby prior to induction?

EBD — Management of cardiovascular related anaesthetic crisis during general anaesthesia

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Differential diagnosis and management of hypotension/ hypertension/arrhythmias intraoperatively

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. If this patient’s blood pressure falls to 70/30mmHg the next time the NIBP cycles, what would you do?
  2. Would your approach be different if this patient’s heart rate increases to 120/min versus 190/min? How would you manage the patient

EBD — Management of ASA 3 or 4 patient presenting for elective or emergency procedures requiring general anaesthesia, e.g., high risk cardiac patient presenting for non-cardiac surgery

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Anaesthetic management of a patient with high cardiac risk (e.g., triple vessel disease that has not been revascularized, ischemic cardiomyopathy with poor ejection fraction, severe aortic stenosis, right ventricular dysfunction) presenting for non-cardiac surgery
  2. Anaesthetic management of a patient with a multi-systemic disease presenting for surgery e.g., rheumatoid arthritis or other autoimmune disease/ metastatic cancer/endocrine disease

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. How will you conduct general anaesthesia for a patient with high cardiac risk for non-cardiac surgery?
  2. Can you explain the anaesthetic considerations for a patient with high cardiac risks and the rationale for your anaesthetic management? How do you optimise the myocardial oxygen supply and demand in a patient with high risk of myocardial ischemia?
  3. Describe your perioperative fluid management plan for a patient with congestive cardiac failure. What are the anaesthetic considerations for a patient with severe valvular heart disease e.g., severe aortic stenosis?
  4. After induction of anaesthesia, you noted that the patient has developed ST depression in lead II on your anaesthesia monitor. Describe how would you manage this patient.
  5. Intraoperatively, you noted that this patient developed fast AF, HR 130s, BP 80/40. Describe your immediate management.
  6. How would you manage a patient on disease modifying anti-rheumatic drugs?
  7. What precautions would you take when positioning the patient with multi-joint arthritis?

EBD — Management of geriatric patient

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Modification of anaesthetic technique taking into account underlying physiological or pathophysiological changes
  2. Optimising provision of GA using monitors like depth of anaesthesia monitoring, regional cerebral oxygen saturation and neuromuscular monitoring
  3. Prevention of postoperative cognitive dysfunction, postoperative delirium, skin injuries, cardiovascular or neurological ischemia

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What is the definition of a geriatric patient?
  2. How would your anaesthetic for this geriatric patient differ from a patient who is 40 years old without significant medical history?
  3. How can we reduce the risk of post-operative cognitive delirium/dysfunction/stroke in this patient through our intraoperative management?
  4. If this patient had moderate dementia, how would your anaesthetic technique differ?
  5. If the PACU nurse calls you to say this patient is agitated and trying to climb out of the trolley, what would you do?
  6. What instruments for assessment of delirium do you know of?

EBD — Management of morbidly obese patient

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Modification of techniques for patients with suspected or confirmed obstructed sleep apnoea (OSA)
  2. Positioning challenges and prevention of position related injuries (staff and patient)
  3. Management of complications like numbness resulting from poor positioning
  4. Anaesthetic considerations for bariatric surgery
  5. Anaesthetic considerations for laparoscopic surgery
  6. Postoperative disposition of patient

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. If this patient has severe OSA, how would your anaesthetic technique be different from if he/she does not have OSA?
  2. What other intraoperative considerations are required for this patient? What precautions should be taken with regards to positioning? How would you manage anaesthetic drug doses?
  3. How is obesity defined?
  4. What are the different types of bariatric procedures that can be performed? What are the criteria for undergoing bariatric surgery? Do you know of any screening tool that would help identify the obese patient at risk of postoperative complications that would benefit from ICU/HD care?
  5. Intraoperatively, what problems do you anticipate with pneumoperitoneum being created during laparoscopy? How would you manage ventilation in the morbidly obese patient in a head down position?

EBD — Management of patient requiring intraoperative neurophysiological monitoring

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Describing the various modes and indications of Intraoperative Neurophysiological Monitoring (IONM)
  2. Adapting anaesthetic management to facilitate various modes of Intraoperative Neurophysiological Monitoring (IONM)
  3. Troubleshooting loss of signal from IONM

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. How would you conduct anaesthesia for a surgery that requires IOMN e.g., SSEP (somatosensory evoked potential) MEP (motor evoked potential), BAEP (Brainstem auditory evoked potential) EMG (electromyography)?
  2. What are the various modes of IONM and their indications for use in surgery? E.g., How are SSEP and MEP performed and what do they monitor intraoperatively?
  3. What are the anaesthesia factors that will affect the IONM readings intraoperatively? Can we use analgesic agents e.g., ketamine or dexmedetomidine in a surgery with IONM?
  4. What would you do if there is loss of IONM signals intraoperatively?
  5. Would your management be different if the signal loss is unilateral or bilateral?

EBD — Management of patient undergoing thoracic surgery requiring one lung ventilation

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Establishing the need for one lung ventilation
  2. Choice of lung isolation technique for surgery
  3. Management of complications during one lung ventilation such as hypoxia, bronchospasm
  4. Anaesthetic considerations for pneumonectomy/ lobectomy/thoracoscopic surgery including choice of anaesthetic agents, fluid management, pain management
  5. Post thoracic surgical complications to watch out for
  6. Extubation strategy

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. Describe how will you conduct anaesthesia for a patient coming for
    1. A video-assisted thoracotomy (VAT) procedure?
    2. Open lung lobectomy?
    3. Open pneumonectomy?

    How will you perform the lung isolation in each of these procedures?
  2. What are the physiological changes during one lung ventilation?
  3. Describe your plans and rationale for fluid management during anaesthesia for a thoracic surgery
  4. What are the analgesic options for this thoracic surgery? Justify your choice.
  5. What are the early post-operative complications that you will look out for after a pneumonectomy?
  6. After positioning and lung isolation, if the patient desaturates to SpO2 85%, what are your differentials and what would you do?
  7. How would you perform lung isolation in a patient with a known difficult airway presenting for a VATS procedure?

EBD — Management of patient undergoing neurosurgical procedure

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Intraoperative management of raised intracranial pressure and ensuring cerebral perfusion, oxygenation and metabolic requirements are met
  2. Additional anaesthetic risks associated with emergency procedures such as aspiration risks and limited time for optimisation
  3. Appropriate disposition for the patient post-operatively
  4. Handing over of care of the patient appropriately including to fellow anaesthetist, nursing staff in post-anaesthesia care unit or staff in the intensive care unit
  5. Management of patient with raised intracranial pressure presenting for neurosurgical procedures with a focus on specific issues such as management of raised intra-cranial pressure, ensuring sufficient cerebral perfusion and minimising secondary brain injury
  6. Additional considerations if patient requires transport to radiology suite

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. How would you provide anaesthesia for an emergency neurosurgery for a patient with raised intracranial pressure?
  2. Can you explain the goals of anaesthesia for this neurosurgery? How does your anaesthetic technique help to achieve this?
  3. The surgeon inserts an ICP monitor intraoperatively, and it reads 40mmHg. Describe your subsequent anaesthetic management to treat this raised ICP.
  4. If the patient requires an urgent procedure at the neuroangio suite (remote location), how would you organise the transport and safe conduct of anaesthesia for a patient with depressed GCS and raised ICP at a remote location?
  5. If the surgeon requires to perform decompressive craniectomy in a prone position, what are your additional anaesthetic considerations?
