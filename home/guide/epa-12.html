EPA 12: Assessing and managing patients with chronic pain

Specifications

  1. Performing assessment with focused history-taking, physical examination, and relevant investigations to ascertain the following:
    • i. Biological basis for pain symptoms
    • ii. Psychological state of the patient
    • iii. Social impact of pain such as ability to function, rest and pursue interests and goals
  2. Diagnosing common chronic pain conditions such as spinal pain, neuropathy
  3. Formulating patient-centric, multimodal, multidisciplinary management chronic pain management plans using the biopsychosocial model approach
  4. Consulting pain specialist or allied health care professionals appropriately
  5. Communicating with patients and their families to allay fears and manage expectations, make informed decisions regarding their chronic pain condition, and counsel regarding treatment strategies including potential side effects of medications or complications of pain treatment procedures
  6. Coordinating care and communicating with all healthcare members using verbal, written or electronic medical record, or other digital technology

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following:

  1. Patients aged less than 16 years old

Sub-competencies: PC2, PC10, MK1, MK2, SBP2, PBL1, P1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Knowledge

  1. Multimodal and multidisciplinary management plan of:
    • a. Nociceptive pain:
    • b. Neuropathic pain
    • c. Inflammatory conditions
    • d. Central sensitisation conditions
  2. Basis of, indications and contra-indications of, effects of and evidence for:
    • a. Pharmacological therapy of neuropathic pain
    • b. Pharmacological therapy of chronic visceral and somatic pain
    • c. Opioid usage in chronic pain
    • d. Interventional pain therapy e.g., Facet joint injection
  3. Knowledge in clinical sciences relevant to pain medicine
  4. Multimodal and multidisciplinary management plan for situations:
    • a. Opioid tolerant, opioid dependent or addicted patients presenting with acute pain
    • b. Altered physiology: Extremes of age (young and old), pregnancy and breastfeeding,
    • c. Co-morbidities such as obstructive sleep apnoea, renal or hepatic impairment.
    • d. Patients with cancer
  5. Biopsychosocial model of pain management
  6. Role of pharmacogenetics in pain management
  7. Role of non-pharmacological therapy such as psychology/ mindfulness

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • Chronic pain history taking including character, severity, impact of pain on function/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations in relation to pain such as MRIs, X –rays, blood investigations (e.g., inflammatory markers, HLA genotyping etc.)
  2. Procedural skills
    • Regional anaesthesia blocks
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient-centric communication including counselling, taking informed consent
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including accurate documentation while maintaining patient confidentiality
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., resource management)

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to experts when appropriate,
  4. Provides efficient patient-centric compassionate care in a timely manner while ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Behaves professionally even under stressful or complex situations
  6. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Chronic pain services including clinics and pain intervention sessions
    • a. minimum of 2 weeks in R1/R2
    • b. minimum of 2 weeks in R3/ Senior Residency (R4 to R5)
  2. Teaching sessions on chronic pain management

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increase in patient dissatisfaction/morbidity including depression
  2. Increase medico-legal liability
  3. Increase healthcare cost
  4. Delay in diagnosis and treatment

Entrustment questions for EPA 12

Mini-CEX — Assessing chronic pain

Discussion points / expectations

  • History and physical examination

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What did you gather after speaking to the patient/family/carer?
  2. What findings did you obtain with clinical examination?
  3. What investigations did you look at or what further investigations would you like to have?
  4. What is your clinical impression/main issues in this case?
  5. What would be the differential diagnosis?
  6. How would you manage this case? What would your management strategy be?
  7. Why did you choose the management you mentioned?
  8. What would you do if your investigations showed critical abnormal findings e.g., new spinal stenosis/disc bulge on MRI?
  9. What are the considerations or your concerns in the management of this case?
  10. What would prompt you to refer to a chronic pain specialist?
  11. What kind of interventions are there for chronic pain that you know of?

EBD — Chronic pain patient coming in for surgery

Discussion points / expectations

  • Pain management for opioid tolerant patient

Suggested entrustment questions (at least 2)

  1. What is your clinical impression/main issues in this case?
  2. What are the considerations or your concerns in the management of this case?
  3. How would you manage this case/What would your management strategy be?
  4. Why did you choose the management you mentioned?
  5. What problems do you anticipate with the patient being on long term opioids or medical cannabis?
