EPA 11: Assessing and managing patients with acute pain

Specifications

  1. Performing assessment using focused history, targeted physical examination and selecting appropriate investigations to gather relevant information
  2. Diagnosing common causes of acute pain based on gathered information
  3. Developing and implementing patient-centric pain management strategies using a multimodal approach, appropriate equipment and techniques based on best available evidence/ guidelines
  4. Discussing with patient +/- family on pain management options and their potential side effects, and setting pain treatment goals together
  5. Documenting all clinical encounters including consent taking for procedures, risk benefit discussions, agreed pain management plan
  6. Coordinating care and communicating with all healthcare members using verbal, written or electronic medical record, or other digital technology
  7. Monitoring for and managing the complications of various acute pain management strategies
  8. Recognising the need to seek senior or specialist opinion/help when appropriate

Limitations

This EPA excludes the following:

  1. Pure chronic pain cases
  2. Patients aged less than 16 years old

Sub-competencies: PC2, PC10, MK1, MK2, SBP2, PBL1, P2, ICS1, ICS2, ICS3

Knowledge

  1. Applied anatomy and physiology of acute pain
  2. Assessment of pain
  3. Pharmacology of analgesics and adjuvants
  4. Role of pain management in surgical outcome/ patient recovery
  5. Multimodal pain management strategies including non-pharmacological options
  6. Equipment for analgesia including catheters, pumps
  7. Management of complications related to pain management
  8. National guidelines on safe prescription of opioids
  9. Organisation and requirements of an institution based acute pain service

Skills

  1. Clinical assessment skills
    • History taking/ physical examination/ ordering and interpretation of relevant investigations in relation to pain
  2. Procedural skills
    • Regional anaesthesia blocks
  3. Communication and interpersonal skills
    • Patient-centric communication including counselling, taking informed consent
    • Healthcare professional/ interdisciplinary centric communication to coordinate multidisciplinary care of patient including accurate documentation while maintaining patient confidentiality
  4. Non-technical skills (e.g., prioritisation, situation awareness, ability to organise, critical decision making, leadership, crisis resource management

Attitudes

  1. Proactive in reflective learning and self- improvement, receptive to feedback
  2. Honest, reliable, conscientious, responsible
  3. Recognises own limits and escalates to supervisor when appropriate,
  4. Provides efficient patient-centric compassionate care in a timely manner while ensuring patient comfort, privacy, and confidentiality
  5. Collegial and respectful team player who is willing to help others

Experiences

  1. Acute Pain service (minimum 1 week/year)
  2. Post Anaesthetic Care Unit during junior residency (minimum 2 weeks/year R1 or R2)
  3. Teaching sessions on pain management
  4. Case log reflections

Potential risks in case of failure

  1. Increase in patient dissatisfaction/morbidity with potential for mortality e.g., opioid overdose
  2. Increase in medico-legal liability
  3. Increase in healthcare cost

Entrustment questions for EPA 11

Mini-CEX — Pain assessment and acute pain management (PACU/Pain rounds)

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Assessment of the patient’s pain via history taking/ physical examination and investigations as appropriate
  2. Management of acute pain

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. What did you gather after speaking to the patient/family/carer?
  2. What findings did you obtain with clinical examination?
  3. What investigations did you look at or what further investigations would you like to have?
  4. What is your clinical impression/main issues in this case?
  5. What would be the differential diagnosis?
  6. How would you manage this case and why did you choose this management strategy?
  7. What are the considerations or your concerns in the management of this case?
  8. Who have you involved or who do you need to involve in your management/communications?
  9. How would you proceed if patient or family do not agree with your plan?
  10. What would you do if patient cannot tolerate the medication you prescribed?
  11. What would you do if your investigations showed critical abnormal findings e.g., new spinal stenosis/disc bulge on MRI?
  12. How would you change your management if the patient has renal dysfunction or on anticoagulants/dual anti-platelets?
  13. How do feel about your management for this case?
  14. How else could you have managed this case differently/better?
  15. Do you feel your experience and knowledge are adequate to manage this case independently? If not, how would you prepare/equip yourself better?

EBD — Management of Blue letter referral for acute pain

Discussion points / expectations

  1. Review by faculty of documentation of blue letter reply by resident with feedback provided by faculty on the quality of reply
  2. Communication with primary team and other members of the healthcare team such as nurses and allied health

Suggested entrustment questions

  1. How did you approach this case?
  2. What did you gather from the history/physical examination?
  3. Would you ask for any investigations and why?
  4. What is your clinical impression/what are the main issues in this case?
  5. What are the considerations for your management strategy?
  6. What would your management strategy be?
  7. What if the primary team/ patient/ family disagrees with your management strategy?
  8. What if the patient is non-verbal but groans constantly?
  9. How should this patient be monitored and followed up?
  10. Are there strategies you know of to prevent the development of chronic pain
